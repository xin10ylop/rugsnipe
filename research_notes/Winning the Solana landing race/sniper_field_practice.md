# Pump.fun creation-slot sniper field practice (2025-2026): architectures, feeds, senders, landing claims, failure modes, and pump.fun protocol changes

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope note: the reader already runs a Rust bot with Yellowstone gRPC, durable nonces, multi-sender fan-out, per-mint compute limits and a Frankfurt plan. These notes focus on what the field does beyond that and what it reports about results. Almost every quantitative claim below comes from a vendor or a bot author with a commercial interest; "measured" vs "marketing" is flagged per item. No Solana Stack Exchange thread on same-slot landing surfaced in any of the searches, and several primary pages (nolimitnodes Medium benchmark, DoubleZero migration page, PumpPortal docs) returned 403/404, so those are recorded as gaps.

## KQ1. Which open-source pump.fun sniper repos are most used, what stack do they use, and do they report landing rates or latency?

### Takeaway
The most-starred repo (chainstacklabs/pumpfun-bonkfun-bot, ~1k stars, Python) is explicitly a learning tool, not a race winner; the serious Rust snipers (D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot, hodlwarden/pumpfun-sniper, 0xfnzero/sol-trade-sdk) converge on the same stack the reader already has (shred feed + gRPC, pre-built tx template patched at fixed offsets, per-provider tip/CU variants, durable nonce per concurrent buy, fan-out to 10-12 senders), and none publishes a measured landing rate; the only numbers are sub-millisecond in-process hot-path timings and a self-reported "70-80% win rate" with no methodology.

### Inferences
- The field's "best practice" open-source stack is essentially what the reader already runs; the deltas are (a) putting the strategy inside the shred-proxy process so detection→send has no IPC hop, (b) emitting on the first shred containing the create tx rather than waiting for full-slot or entry reconstruction, (c) a boot-time latency gate that drops senders slower than ~20 ms, and (d) a filter on dev-buy size (0.5-4.5 SOL window) as a cheap pre-trade screen.
- Star counts are a poor proxy for competitiveness: the 1k-star repo is pedagogical; the repos that describe real race engineering have 8-63 stars. The high-star "sniper" repos with keyword-stuffed READMEs and unverifiable win rates should be treated as lead generation for private-key-collecting or paid-support schemes (the Chainstack README warns its Issues tab "is targeted by scam bots").

### Gaps
- No open-source repo publishes a measured landing rate, slot-offset histogram, or detect-to-land latency. The only hard numbers are in-process (µs) timings.
- GitHub's search API was blocked in this environment (repo-scoped token), so star rankings beyond the first 20 of the `pumpdotfun` topic page and `shredstream` topic were not retrieved.

## KQ3. How do bots detect a create (gRPC, shreds, websockets, PumpPortal) and what do public comparisons say about time-to-detect?

### Takeaway
Shred feeds beat Yellowstone gRPC by tens to low-hundreds of ms in every published comparison (Dysnix: 64.5% first, 32.8 ms mean lead; RPC Fast: ~120 ms mean, 270 ms tail; OrbitFlare vs Jito shreds: 6.5 ms median), but the free Jito ShredStream was deprecated in July 2026 and shut down September 5 2026, so the 2026 field has moved to DoubleZero Edge or paid vendor shred feeds (OrbitFlare, AllenHark "Jito Shreds", DedicatedNodes) or own-validator shreds.

### Cited Findings
- Jito docs: "We are beginning the process of deprecating Jito ShredStream." "The service will be completely shut down in 60 days" (September 5, 2026); "we highly recommend transitioning to DoubleZero Edge before the shutdown date"; migration guide/free trial at doublezero.xyz/jito-shredstream. Proxy details: auth keypair must be pre-approved, `DESIRED_REGIONS` "maximum of 2 regions", default `20000/udp`, "NAT connections currently not supported", optional `GRPC_SERVICE_PORT` deshreds to a gRPC stream; marketing claim "save hundreds of milliseconds during trading on Solana" — [Jito ShredStream docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/)
- OrbitFlare (Jul 27 2026): "Jito published the deprecation notice in early July"; "The service shuts down completely on September 5, 2026." OrbitFlare's own Frankfurt benchmark vs Jito ShredStream: "Over 10,014 transactions, our Shredstream was first 95.93% of the time"; "When Jito's feed was behind, it was behind by 6.51ms at the median and 28.77ms at p99." Offers raw UDP shreds in nine regions and a deshredded gRPC "Jetstream" — [OrbitFlare migration guide](https://orbitflare.com/blog/developers/jito-shredstream-shutdown-migration-guide)
- Conflicting status report: one vendor post says "the free Jito ShredStream closed on 5 September 2026; Jito ShredStream itself still runs, but access to it now sits behind DoubleZero" (search summary; the underlying Medium post returned 403) — [nolimitnodes Medium](https://medium.com/@nolimitnodes/shredstream-vs-yellowstone-grpc-on-solana-where-the-milliseconds-actually-go-0faabe6f2b9d); replacement vendors: [AllenHark Jito Shreds](https://allenhark.com/infrastructure/jito-shreds), [DedicatedNodes](https://www.dedicatednodes.io/jito-shredstream-alternative/)
- Dysnix benchmark (search summary of the AI-agents post): 2,078,707 matched transactions; ShredStream arrived first in 64.5% of cases with a 32.8 ms average lead and peaks at 1,323 ms — [Dysnix low-latency infra](https://dysnix.com/blog/how-to-build-low-latency-infrastructure-for-solana-ai-agents)
- RPC Fast docs: ShredStream on a node that also feeds Yellowstone improved arrival by "an average advantage of 120ms and up to 270ms at the tail"; a sibling page says "~50-100ms earlier on average"; another vendor page (Dysnix HFT) states "~2 minutes earlier on average", which is internally inconsistent with its own "100-150ms earlier" and should be discarded — [RPC Fast ShredStream docs](https://docs.rpcfast.com/solana/jito-shredstream-grpc); [Dysnix HFT](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-rpc-strategy-and-infrastructure-for-hft-bots)
- RPC Fast latency budget (vendor): polling RPC detection 100-500 ms; WebSocket 150-300 ms; Yellowstone gRPC 5-20 ms; "ShredStream lookahead: 50-200 ms"; decision <5 ms; tx construction <5 ms; signing <1 ms; multi-relay submission 5-30 ms; detection-to-submission target <50 ms; hot path <5 ms ("AllenHark floor: sub-1 ms; most production bots: 2-5 ms"); cross-region penalty 80-150 ms minimum — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)
- Dysnix: Yellowstone gRPC "Roughly 50-100ms faster than standard RPC subscriptions"; WebSockets "typically deliver data 150-300ms behind the actual on-chain event"; bloXroute OFR "30-50ms" expected improvement over local Geyser nodes; "According to our tests, Jito performs better than bloXroute OFR (as of summer 2025)" (no numbers) — [Dysnix HFT](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-rpc-strategy-and-infrastructure-for-hft-bots)
- GetBlock guide: Yellowstone gRPC ~400 ms vs 1-3 s WebSocket vs 2-5 s polling (vendor rough numbers) — [GetBlock guide](https://docs.getblock.io/guides/tracking-pump.fun-token-mints-in-real-time-with-getblocks-yellowstone-grpc)
- Chainstack Geyser listener: subscribes to successful txs touching the pump.fun program and checks "the first 8 bytes of instruction data" for the Create discriminator; "Geyser is consistently the front-runner"; "The absolute numbers depend on your provider and network path"; no published ms figures — [Chainstack Geyser doc](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-listening-to-pumpfun-token-mint-using-geyser)
- hodlwarden: shreds lack tx meta, so he pairs ShredStream (detect) with gRPC (meta/confirm); D3AD-E emits on the first shred carrying the create tx bytes rather than after full reconstruction — [hodlwarden](https://github.com/hodlwarden/pumpfun-sniper); [D3AD-E](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot)
- Helius LaserStream positions itself as "shred-level ingestion" and a Yellowstone drop-in; its latency guide says measurements are meaningful only when "co-locating your testing infrastructure in the same region as your chosen LaserStream endpoint"; mainnet LaserStream requires Business/Professional plan — [Helius measuring latency](https://docs.helius.xyz/docs/laserstream/guides/measuring-latency); [Helius zero-slot](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot)
- The Chainstack README notes a structural blind spot of shred-level detection: the `shreds` listener "cannot see router-created coins" (creates routed through another program) — [chainstacklabs](https://github.com/chainstacklabs/pumpfun-bonkfun-bot)

### Inferences
- For a bot on Yellowstone gRPC only, the field's consensus is that it is 30-150 ms behind shred-fed competitors on detection, which in a 400 ms slot is the difference between same-slot and next-slot. Post-September 2026 the practical options are DoubleZero Edge, a paid shred vendor, or running an own (unstaked is fine) validator/Agave node for Turbine shreds.
- Shred-based detection needs a fallback (gRPC) both for meta and for creates that arrive via routers/CPI, which D3AD-E and hodlwarden both implement.

### Gaps
- No independent, non-vendor time-to-detect comparison exists; every benchmark is by a seller of the faster feed. Pricing/benchmarks of DoubleZero Edge could not be retrieved (403).
- PumpPortal docs returned 404; no latency claim for PumpPortal's `subscribeNewToken` websocket was retrieved. It is a hosted websocket, so by the WebSocket figures above it is at least 150-300 ms behind gRPC.

## KQ4. What is the consensus on same-slot ("block 0") landing?

### Takeaway
No source claims same-slot landing is reliably achievable by reaction (seeing the create and then sending); vendors frame it as a goal that requires shred visibility plus a leader-aware relay with staked connections, and the one measurement that exists (Pine Analytics, Apr 2025) shows that >50% of pump.fun tokens had a same-block buyer, overwhelmingly deployer-coordinated wallets, i.e. pre-arranged bundles rather than reactive snipes.

### Cited Findings
- Pine Analytics (Apr 21 2025): "over 50% of tokens" were sniped in their creation block (all launches); same-block participation "typically requires" pre-signed transactions, off-chain coordination, or shared infrastructure between deployer and buyer; distinguishes "Spray-and-pray bots, likely testing heuristics or dusting" from "Coordinated insiders" — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- AllenHark (Nov 20 2025): same-slot is a goal, "get it to the leader while slot `N` is still being produced"; "The relay follows the leader schedule and knows which validator is producing now"; Jito "adds its own steps" (receive, simulate, forward), its auction "can become a bottleneck" and "is not built for reacting inside the current slot"; advice "Run your bot logic on co-located servers in Frankfurt, near the relay and the data feeds"; no landing-rate data — [AllenHark same-slot](https://allenhark.com/blog/how-to-land-solana-transaction-same-slot)
- RPC Fast (Dec 2025, updated Sep 2026): "a single polished execution path" degrades under volatility; in "memecoin and airdrop frenzies" same-block trades slip to the next slot; swQoS "materially reduces time to inclusion, especially for 'slow' users"; never states same-block is guaranteed for non-validators — [RPC Fast pillars](https://rpcfast.com/blog/pillars-of-choosing-a-solana-rpc-provider-for-trading-bots)
- Dysnix: "83% first-block hit rate thanks to stake-weighted QoS" vs "sub-40% first-block rates without SWQoS during congestion" (no method); SWQoS "gives a stake-weighted priority to 80% of a leader's TPU capacity"; "Direct TPU saves maybe 10-20ms but requires significantly more infrastructure code" — [Dysnix HFT](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-rpc-strategy-and-infrastructure-for-hft-bots)
- Helius: same-slot is conditional ("could land in the same slot"), substantial stake and staked routing are "the most important factor" in time to inclusion; Helius is "the network's largest validator by stake" — [Helius zero-slot](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot)
- Jito bundle semantics: "Bundles execute within the same slot"; auctions "run at 50ms ticks"; a success response "does not guarantee the bundle will be processed or land on-chain"; default rate limit "1 request per second per IP per region" — [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- Nozomi is the only sender advertising median same-slot (0 slots median, 1 slot p99) with a stated caveat about client quality — [Temporal Nozomi](https://temporal.xyz/services/nozomi)
- Bundler vendors state the creator's atomic bundle cannot be beaten: snipers "cannot insert transactions ahead of an atomic bundle" and "can still buy from block 1 onward" (vendor position) — [SolBundler comparison](https://solbundler.app/blog/pump-fun-sniper-bots-comparison-2026)

### Inferences
- "Block 0" in field usage conflates two things: (a) being inside the creator's bundle (only possible by coordination with the deployer), and (b) landing a reactive buy in the create slot behind the bundle. Evidence for (b) is limited to vendor claims (Nozomi median-0, RPC Fast 89% on migrations); none is from a contested pump.fun create. The honest field position is that (b) is possible for a shred-fed, colocated bot sending through staked/leader-aware relays, with next-slot being the realistic median under contention.
- The 2025 Pine figure (>50% of tokens have a same-block buyer) means most of the "slot-0 competition" is not racing the reader at all; it is pre-arranged. A reactive bot's real adversaries in slot 0 are the handful of other shred-fed bots.

### Gaps
- No Solana Stack Exchange thread or Agave-level write-up on reactive same-slot landing was found with the available search tools.
- No published slot-offset distribution (slot 0 / +1 / +2 shares) for reactive pump.fun create snipes by any non-insider bot.

## KQ5. Pre-signing when the mint is unknown, durable nonces, variants, detect-to-send times

### Takeaway
Every documented design pre-builds a template with placeholder mint/ATA/curve accounts, patches bytes at known offsets after decoding the create, and signs per sender variant on its own thread; durable nonces are used so one logical buy fans out safely with "at most one lands"; reported in-process detect-to-send is 5-20 µs (D3AD-E) to "sub-1 ms" (AllenHark), with the rest of the budget being network.

### Cited Findings
- D3AD-E: pre-built tx with accounts "patched at fixed byte offsets", token-account seeds derived at startup, per-provider "tip account, tip and CU price" stamped then signed (~10 µs); `on_create` 5-7 µs; one durable nonce per concurrent buy; "at most one lands" — [D3AD-E](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot)
- AllenHark: "Signs a pre-built transaction with the new Mint Address"; blockhash cached and refreshed every 400 ms (no nonces); sample relay packet over QUIC; detection decodes the Create instruction to extract the mint — [AllenHark deep-dive](https://allenhark.com/blog/pumpfun-sniper-technical-deep-dive); [AllenHark Rust guide](https://allenhark.com/blog/so-you-wanna-build-pumpfun-sniper)
- RPC Fast: pre-build "instruction templates, Address Lookup Tables, a signature payload structure, a signed tip transaction, and a pre-loaded keypair. Only the mint and amount are filled at fire time"; recommends simulateBundle before every paid submission ("free and catches most state-mismatch failures"); describes multi-relay fan-out of the same bundle rather than multiple tx variants — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)
- sol-trade-sdk: durable nonce required for multi-service sends; ALTs to shrink tx; seed optimization and pre-created ATAs; `HotPathMinimal` account policy; first accepted result returns early — [sol-trade-sdk](https://github.com/0xfnzero/sol-trade-sdk)
- Durable-nonce caveats from a Bloom design issue: a trade pre-signed against the bonding curve "dies if the coin graduates to PumpSwap"; "The priority fee and Jito tip are fixed at signing"; "A durable-nonce transaction that fails on-chain still advances the nonce and burns the order" — [bloom-petal-pumpfun issue #10](https://github.com/bloom-directory/bloom-petal-pumpfun/issues/10)
- Pump.fun dev channel warning relevant to templates: "Do not use cached addresses for bonding_curve.creator or pool.coin_creator" after the Jan 9 creator_vault change; integrators must fetch the latest creator on-chain — [pump_tech_updates](https://t.me/s/pump_tech_updates)
- Helius: skipping preflight saves "upwards of 100ms"; Sender requires `skipPreflight: true`, `maxRetries: 0` — [Helius zero-slot](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot)
- Jito: for `sendTransaction` recommend a 70/30 split between priority fee and Jito tip; pick tip account "at random to reduce contention"; do not put tip accounts in ALTs — [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)

### Inferences
- Nobody fully pre-signs before the create is seen; "pre-signed" in the literature means the ~10 µs sign after a byte patch. The reader's approach matches. The only additional trick seen is deriving all PDAs/ATA seeds and preparing per-sender tip/CU variants on dedicated threads so the post-detect critical path is patch+sign+send only.
- The creator_vault derivation now depends on on-chain creator state (can be changed post-launch and shared among up to 10 wallets), so a buy template must read `creator` from the create tx itself, not from a cache; a stale creator_vault is a plausible silent failure mode for templated buys in 2026.

### Gaps
- No source reports measured end-to-end detect-to-land (not detect-to-send) for a pump.fun create. The microsecond figures stop at the NIC.
- No source quantifies how often durable-nonce buys fail because of the nonce being advanced by a losing variant that executed-and-failed on-chain (vs. never landing).

## KQ6. Handling the creator's atomic bundle: inside, behind, or next slot?

### Takeaway
Field consensus is that a reactive sniper cannot get inside or ahead of a Jito create+buy bundle; it lands behind it in the same slot at best (and Jito's `jitodontfront` convention does not block that back-run), otherwise in slot N+1; the creator-side counter is to not bundle buys at all and instead dump on snipers.

### Cited Findings
- Jito `jitodontfront`: add "any valid Solana public key that starts with jitodontfront to any of the instructions"; "Any bundle containing a transaction with the jitodontfront account will be rejected by the block engine unless that transaction appears first (at index 0) in the bundle"; allowed `[tx_with_dont_front, tip]` and `[tx_with_dont_front, arbitrage, tip]`; blocked `[tip, tx_with_dont_front]`; "may help reduce sandwich attacks but is not guaranteed"; block-engine-only enforcement — [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- RPC Fast describes "A jitodontfront-prefixed account in the first transaction, to block competing back-running bundles" (this conflicts with Jito's own rule, which only forbids anything before the protected tx) — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)
- cicere/pumpfun-bundler (438 stars, updated 03.02.2026): "Launch Pump" buys from all sub-wallets "simultaneously at block 0" and claims "maximum protection against front-running, MEV, and snipers"; "Magic Mode" (create then immediately buy with sub-wallets) "Can be front-run by other snipers but often achieves better entry prices"; "Launch + Snipe V2" buys with dev wallet in one tx then sends snipe bundles from sub-wallets so buys "won't be flagged as 'Rats/Insiders'"; "Sniper Dump" is a "Clever anti-sniper tactic that launches with only the dev wallet buying" then triggers a 100% sell when buy volume hits a threshold; uses regional Jito block engines and a custom LUT; up to 20 (About says 25) buyer keypairs — [cicere/pumpfun-bundler](https://github.com/cicere/pumpfun-bundler)
- SolBundler: snipers "cannot insert transactions ahead of an atomic bundle"; "can still buy from block 1 onward" — [SolBundler comparison](https://solbundler.app/blog/pump-fun-sniper-bots-comparison-2026)
- Jito bundles are up to 5 txs, sequential, atomic, "execute within the same slot" — [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- Pump.fun's own "synthetic migration" (2026 release): the final bonding-curve buy "fills the curve first, then buys against AMM reserves without buy caps", which removes the migration gap that migration snipers targeted — [pump_tech_updates](https://t.me/s/pump_tech_updates)

### Inferences
- Because shreds carry the creator's full signed create tx, a sniper could in principle rebuild a Jito bundle `[creator_create_tx, my_buy, tip]`; Jito's rule permits that ordering even with `jitodontfront`. But if the create is already inside the creator's own bundle with their buys, the sniper's bundle would duplicate the create and fail, so in practice the sniper's buy must be a standalone tx that executes after the creator's bundle in the same slot or the next. This is an inference from the documented rules, not something any source describes doing.
- The "Magic Mode can be front-run by other snipers" admission is the field's clearest statement that when create and dev-buy are separate transactions, shred-fed snipers do sometimes land between them in the create slot.

### Gaps
- No data on how often reactive snipers land in the create slot behind a bundle vs. in N+1; no source measured position-within-slot.

## KQ7. What went wrong for sniper operators (bans, rate limits, duplicate fills, stuck positions, anti-sniper traps) and what does the data say about creation-slot buyer profitability?

### Takeaway
The only quantitative profitability study of same-block buyers (Pine Analytics, Apr 2025) shows 87% profitable snipes and >15,000 SOL/month extracted, but only for deployer-funded wallets; no study isolates non-insider reactive snipers, and the creator-side playbook in 2026 (dev-only buy then dump on sniper volume, multi-wallet bundles that look organic, one-time creator-fee edits) is explicitly designed to make reactive slot-0 buyers the exit liquidity.

### Cited Findings
- Pine Analytics (Apr 21 2025; window Mar 15 to publication): filter = same-block snipe plus one-hop SOL transfer between deployer and sniper; 15,000+ tokens (~1.75% of launch activity), 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers, "more than 15,000 SOL" realized profit, 87% profitable snipes, typical 1-100 SOL per wallet with outliers >500 SOL; exits: >55% fully out in under one minute, ~85% within five minutes, >11% in 15 s or less; >90% sold in one or two swaps; activity clustered 14:00-23:00 UTC; multi-hop funding ("often 5 to 7 hops deep") not captured; no profit figures for non-insider bots — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Secondary coverage of the same report describes the pattern: deployers "fund one or more sniper wallets and grant them advance notice of upcoming token launches", then "dump tokens quickly" — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-meme-coin-snipers-systematic-problem/); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2185070)
- CoinGecko (all traders, realized PnL only, bots not filtered): profitable-wallet share bottomed at 30.1% in June 2025 and reached 73.3% in April 2026; "Bagholders are excluded" — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)
- Dune-based report: only ~0.4% of wallets realized >$10,000; ~293 wallets >$1M — [Cryptometer](https://www.cryptometer.io/news/pump-fun-data-shows-most-traders-havent-locked-major-gains/)
- Vendor failure stats (unverified): "about 90% of DIY sniper bots fail from latency leaks or rug filters" (RPC Fast 2025); "over 70% of bots target sub-50 ms RPC latency, but about 10% deliver consistent profits" (Dysnix Jan 2026) — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)
- Anti-sniper economics (scorplabs): a bundler spending 10 SOL at block zero "owns ~26.6% of supply and is back to break-even after roughly 0.6 SOL of organic buying"; "nothing here is technically a rug: no authority is abused, no liquidity is stolen" — [scorplabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-bundle-detection)
- Anti-sniper tactics sold openly: cicere "Sniper Dump" (dev-only buy, "instant 100% dump when volume reaches your configured threshold"), generated wallet profiles and "varied" holding histories meant to "pass the sniff test", "Smart Sell" that "sells a configured percentage of matching volume" — [cicere/pumpfun-bundler](https://github.com/cicere/pumpfun-bundler)
- Claimed platform-level anti-bot changes (single unsourced article, Apr 20 2026): "Fair Launch Shield" with "CAPTCHA-gated launch windows and configurable anti-bot settings", creator buys spread "across up to 17 wallets in a single transaction", March 2026 cap of creator fee modifications "to a single post-launch edit", weekly revenue "falling 80% from peak in some reporting windows" in Feb 2026; the article cites no sources and none of these appear in pump.fun's developer channel — [bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/04/20/meme-launchpad-2-pump-fun-letsbonk-anti-sniper-reputation)
- The pump.fun developer channel does document a one-time creator reward distribution policy ("Reward distribution can be configured only once"; RevokeFeeSharingAuthority/TransferFeeSharingAuthority "no longer supported"), which matches the "single post-launch edit" claim in substance — [pump_tech_updates](https://t.me/s/pump_tech_updates)
- Operational failure modes documented by vendors: a failed Jito bundle "still pays the tip"; "Tip floors can rise 10-50x in the seconds around a migration"; Jito default rate limit "1 request per second per IP per region" with 429s; Helius Sender default 6 TPS; Jito `sendBundle` success "does not guarantee" landing; durable-nonce order "dies if the coin graduates to PumpSwap" and a failing nonce tx "still advances the nonce" — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump); [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/); [Helius zero-slot](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot); [bloom-petal issue #10](https://github.com/bloom-directory/bloom-petal-pumpfun/issues/10)
- Mayhem Mode stuck-position risk (pump.fun docs, updated Nov 12 2025): if the Agent is a net seller and holders sell, "there may be some holders who cannot sell their tokens into the bonding curve"; "Mayhem Mode Agents will not pay pump.fun protocol fees"; agent trades "with equal probabilities in a random walk" for the first 24 hours; unsold agent tokens burned; total supply 2,000,000,000 — [pump.fun Mayhem Mode docs](https://pump.fun/docs/mayhem-mode)
- Mayhem first-week reception: daily launches rose only from ~17,300 to ~17,800 while trading revenues "have fallen 40-50%" — [crypto-economy](https://crypto-economy.com/pump-funs-mayhem-mode-records-underwhelming-first-week-performance/)
- AllenHark's practical mitigations: dev-buy window filter (0.5-4.5 SOL), spam filter on account count, auto-blacklist creators after a loss >0.001 SOL, blacklist reload every 10 s — [AllenHark deep-dive](https://allenhark.com/blog/pumpfun-sniper-technical-deep-dive)

### Inferences
- Every profitability number for slot-0 buyers that exists is for insiders. The absence of any published positive track record for reactive non-insider creation-slot sniping, combined with vendors' own "~10% of bots profit" statements and the openly sold "Sniper Dump" tactic, is itself a finding: the field's reported experience of reactive slot-0 buying is that it is the counterparty the creator's tooling is optimized against.
- The dev-buy-size filter (both AllenHark and hodlwarden's "low-tip transaction detection") is the field's main cheap screen; "Sniper Dump" launches (dev-only buy, no bundle) will pass a "no bundle" heuristic and are exactly the trap, so bundle-presence alone is not a safety signal in either direction.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies bans/account suspensions by sender vendors, duplicate-fill rates, or stuck-position frequency for snipers.
- The "Fair Launch Shield"/CAPTCHA claims are from one unsourced article and are not in pump.fun's developer channel; treat as unconfirmed. Pump.fun's own docs for Mayhem Mode mention no anti-bot intent.

## KQ9. Tricks to cut failed-attempt cost

### Takeaway
The field's cost controls are: pay-on-land tips (Nozomi), one durable nonce per logical buy so fan-out costs at most one landing, tight per-sender tip caps and dynamic tips from tip-floor streams, free simulation before paid bundles, skip-preflight with zero retries, dev-buy-size and account-count filters to avoid firing at all, and a ~20 ms endpoint gate so slow senders never get a copy.

### Cited Findings
- Nozomi "Pay-on-land tip model" (tips charged only on landing); live tip floor websocket — [Temporal Nozomi](https://temporal.xyz/services/nozomi)
- D3AD-E: `SNIPER_TIP_MIN_SOL`/`MAX`/`DYNAMIC_TIP`, `SNIPER_CU_LIMIT`, endpoint gate default 20 ms, "at most one lands" per nonce — [D3AD-E](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot)
- RPC Fast: simulateBundle before every paid submission; a failed bundle still pays the tip; tip floors 10-50x around hot events — [RPC Fast snipe guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)
- Helius: `skipPreflight: true`, `maxRetries: 0`; tips from Jito tip-floor API 75th percentile plus buffer; SWQoS-only tier at 5,000 lamports — [Helius zero-slot](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot)
- Jito: minimum bundle tip 1,000 lamports; for sendTransaction split 70/30 priority fee/tip; "Tips to non-Jito-Solana leaders are ineffective" — [Jito send docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- Dysnix: Jito tips "0.001-0.01 SOL" in moderate activity, "can exceed 0.1 SOL" in extreme congestion; default 200,000 CU per instruction — [Dysnix HFT](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-rpc-strategy-and-infrastructure-for-hft-bots)
- hodlwarden: "Low-tip transaction detection (configurable threshold)" used as a filter on the creator's tx — [hodlwarden](https://github.com/hodlwarden/pumpfun-sniper)
- Chainstack: dynamic priority fee costs an extra RPC call; fixed-token-amount "extreme fast mode" removes the quote step — [chainstacklabs](https://github.com/chainstacklabs/pumpfun-bonkfun-bot)

### Inferences
- Beyond what the reader runs, the two field practices worth noting are pay-on-land senders (shifts failed-attempt cost to zero for that lane) and reading the creator's own tip/priority fee from the create tx as a signal of how contested the slot will be before deciding to fire.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies failed-attempt cost per launch or the share of tips burned on non-landing variants.
