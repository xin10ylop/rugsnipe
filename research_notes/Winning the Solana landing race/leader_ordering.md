# Leader ingestion and intra-block ordering on Solana (Oct 2026) — from the point of view of a bot that wants to be the first buy after an atomic Jito bundle

Scope note: everything below is current to 2026-10-18 unless flagged. Two facts change the framing of the assignment itself and are repeated where relevant:
(a) the mainnet slot is **200 ms, not 400 ms** since epoch 1053 (2026-10-09) under SIMD-0525, and the leader window is still 4 slots (so 800 ms of wall clock per leader);
(b) **Alpenglow activates at epoch 1064 (~2026-10-21 14:50 UTC)**; it changes consensus/pacing, not the scheduler, but it forces every leader onto Agave-4.3-based clients (Agave / Jito-Solana / Harmonic Salsa), takes Firedancer and Frankendancer off the leader set during migration, and puts BAM nodes into maintenance mode around activation.

## Q1. Direct-to-leader submission: TPU over QUIC, SWQoS, connection/stream limits, when to connect, leader window, "current + next leader"

### Takeaway
An unstaked sender (no delegated stake behind its identity) gets a QUIC connection with 128 concurrent streams, a 40-streams-per-100 ms quota (10 when the leader is loaded), shares a public pool (500 slots pre-3.1, 2 000 in 3.1, 3 000 on master) that is pruned oldest-first and capped at 8 connections per IP per minute; staked identities get a separate 2 000-slot pool, a stake-proportional stream quota (and no TPS throttling at all under normal load in Agave 4.0), and are never pruned by age. Anza's own tool (Feb 2026) measured ~0.29 slots mean landing latency and ~2.3 % loss from Amsterdam over a staked connection sending to the current leader in slots 1–3 of the window and to current+next in slot 4, with connections opened 4–8 slots ahead.

### Cited Findings
- SWQoS gives stake-weighted priority to "80% of a leader's TPU capacity"; the 80 % is split in proportion to lamports in `--staked-nodes-overrides` plus cluster stake; it only works if both the validator (`--staked-nodes-overrides`) and the RPC (`--rpc-send-transaction-tpu-peer HOST:PORT`) are configured; "RPCs should not be staked in any way"; a 0.5 % stake can send "up to 0.5% of packets" — [Solana docs: SWQoS guide](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/stake-weighted-qos)
- Agave master `streamer/src/quic.rs` defaults: `DEFAULT_MAX_STAKED_CONNECTIONS = 2000`, `DEFAULT_MAX_UNSTAKED_CONNECTIONS = 3000`, `DEFAULT_MAX_QUIC_CONNECTIONS_PER_UNSTAKED_PEER = 8`, `DEFAULT_MAX_QUIC_CONNECTIONS_PER_STAKED_PEER = 16`, `DEFAULT_MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_IPADDR_PER_MINUTE = 8`, `DEFAULT_MAX_STREAMS_PER_MS = 500` ("Limit to 500K PPS"), `QUIC_MAX_TIMEOUT = 30 s` idle, stake revalidation every 1–2 h — [agave streamer/src/quic.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/streamer/src/quic.rs)

### Inferences
- A Frankfurt bot with no stake can connect to the leader's TPU and will usually be accepted (the 8-per-IP-per-minute and 3 000-slot public pool are generous at rest), but under load its per-connection quota collapses to 10 streams/100 ms (or 0 under Agave 4.1's proposed rule) and its connection is first in line for oldest-first pruning. For a one-packet-at-the-right-moment use case the binding constraint is not throughput but eviction/handshake risk: keep ≤8 connections per IP, open them ≥4 slots (0.8 s) ahead, keep them alive (30 s idle timeout), and never reconnect in a tight loop.
- Because a leader's window is now 800 ms and a pump.fun create arrives at some random point inside it, the "current + next leader" fanout only matters in the last slot of the window; for a same-slot race, only the **current** leader matters, and the packet must reach it well inside the remaining portion of the 200 ms slot (see Q7).
- Helius' 10 000-lamport fee floor and "validators block low-fee transactions" mean that any commercial staked path needs a non-trivial priority fee attached even when ordering is not fee-driven.

### Gaps
- No primary source on Firedancer's SWQoS/unstaked policy; `default.toml` only exposes `max_concurrent_connections = 131072`, `max_concurrent_handshakes = 4096`, `idle_timeout_millis = 10000` with no stake comments — [firedancer default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/app/fdctl/config/default.toml). Third-party READMEs say Firedancer also implements SWQoS and drops unstaked first under load, unverified.
- No measurement of unstaked-vs-staked landing rates for a single low-rate sender in 2026 (Anza measured staked only; Chorus One is 2024 and confounded).
- Exact mainnet Agave version mix (3.1 vs 4.0 vs 4.3) in October 2026 not found; the Triton PR numbers were not checked against release notes.

## Q2. Agave 2.x/3.x/4.x: how priority is computed, how the (greedy) scheduler batches, how mid-slot arrivals are placed, and how non-strict the ordering is

### Takeaway
Since v1.18 (default since v2.0) Agave ranks by `priority = reward × 1 000 000 / (cost + 1)` where reward = full prioritization fee (price × requested CU limit) + the validator's unburned 50 % of the base fee, and cost = the cost model's estimate (requested CU limit + signature + write-lock + data costs), so requesting fewer CUs raises rank at a fixed CU price; the greedy scheduler (default since v2.3) pops in priority order, assigns to one of several worker threads under a linearly paced CU budget, and ordering within the block is only approximately by priority because of multi-threading, batching, lock conflicts and pacing.

### Cited Findings
- Agave v3.1.8 `calculate_priority_and_cost`: doc "P = R / (1 + C)"; `const MULTIPLIER: u64 = 1_000_000`; `cost = CostModel::calculate_cost(transaction, &bank.feature_set).sum()`; `reward = bank.calculate_reward_for_transaction(transaction, fee_budget_limits)`; "Current minimum costs are on the order of several hundred" — [agave v3.1.8 receive_and_buffer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/v3.1.8/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/receive_and_buffer.rs)
- Solana docs (v3.1.8): "Priority = reward * 1,000,000 / (cost + 1)"; reward is "the prioritization fee plus the portion of the base fee that is not burned" (burn is 50 %; under SIMD-0096 the full prioritization fee goes to the validator); cost = signature + write lock + instruction data + program execution + loaded-accounts-data-size costs; the prioritization fee is `compute_unit_price × compute_unit_limit / 1e6` rounded up, "based on the requested CU limit, not actual CU usage" — [solana.com fee-structure](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/fee-structure.md)
- Anza v1.18 announcement: priority changed from plain fee-per-CU to a formula incorporating the base fee — [Anza central scheduler post](https://anza.xyz/blog/introducing-the-central-scheduler-an-optional-feature-of-agave-v1-18); Solana forum write-up gives the same thing as "Priority = ((priority fee × requested CUs) + base fee) / (1 + requested CUs)" — [Solana forum, May 2026](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803)
- Default scheduler history: central (prio-graph) scheduler default in v2.0 — [Helius Agave v2](https://docs.helius.dev/blog/agave-v2-update); "central-scheduler-greedy is now activated by default" in v2.3 — [Anza 2.3 patch notes](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-2-3-patch-notes-whats-coming); Agave 3.0 adds `TransactionView` (zero-copy parsing) and SIMD-0083 allows conflicting txs in the same entry, executed "sequentially in the order they appear in the entry" — [Helius Agave 3.0](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v3-0)
- Greedy scheduler (master): "Dead-simple scheduler that is efficient and will attempt to schedule in priority order, scheduling anything that can be immediately scheduled, up to the limits"; `target_scheduled_cus = MAX_BLOCK_UNITS / 4` per pass, split per thread; `max_scanned_transactions_per_scheduling_pass = 100_000`; batch target `DEFAULT_TARGET_ENTRY_BYTES_PER_BATCH` = 15 % of a shred batch; a thread stops receiving work when its channel is full or in-flight CUs reach its target; conflicting-but-unschedulable txs go to `unschedulables` and are pushed back onto the queue (not dropped); test `test_schedule_scan_past_highest_priority` shows a lower-priority tx that conflicts only with a blocked higher-priority tx is scheduled anyway ("Without priority guarding, it will be scheduled") — [agave greedy_scheduler.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/greedy_scheduler.rs)
- Scheduler controller loop (master): per tick order is decision → receive_completed → bank-transition check → drain check results → **schedule** → (recheck if no bank) → **receive_and_buffer**; new scheduling on a new bank waits for in-flight work (`WaitingForInFlight`, "A slot transition gates only new scheduling"); `CostPacer` paces the block CU budget linearly: `allocation_per_milli = block_limit / fill_time`, with `DEFAULT_SCHEDULER_PACING_NON_FILL_TIME_MILLIS = 50` and fill time = `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT − 50` (clamped to the slot time if larger); after fill time the whole remaining limit is available; `Forward` clears the container — [agave scheduler_controller.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/scheduler_controller.rs)
- Receive path (master): `RECV_TIMEOUT = 10 ms` blocking receive only when the container is empty and the node is not leader; when leader (`Consume`) receive is non-blocking (`try_recv`) up to `MAX_PACKET_RECEIVES = 512` per call; pre-checks: sanitize/ALT resolve, compute-budget, max age (`max_processing_age`), fee-payer; nonce dedup keeps the higher-priority tx; container overflow drops with `num_dropped_on_capacity` — [agave receive_and_buffer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/receive_and_buffer.rs)
- Thread affinity rule (forum summary of ThreadAwareAccountLocks): "If a transaction needs locks already held by a thread, it is placed on that thread. Otherwise, any available thread can take it"; blocked high-priority tx stays in the graph and lower-priority conflicting txs are held back (prio-graph variant) — [Solana forum](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803)
- Helius (older): 4 non-vote banking threads each with a local batch of up to 64 non-conflicting txs; packets batched in 128s; "The v1.18 client gives higher priority to transactions that require fewer CUs"; default CU limit 200 000 if unset — [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/how-to-land-transactions-on-solana)
- Chorus One: within the banking stage transactions are "non-deterministically assigned to queues within different execution threads"; a higher fee "doesn't guarantee" first execution — [Chorus One](https://chorus.one/reports-research/transaction-latency-on-solana-do-swqos-priority-fees-and-jito-tips-make-your-transactions-land-faster)

### Inferences
- For two competing pump.fun buys with the same CU price, the one with the smaller requested CU limit ranks higher only marginally: reward scales with the limit (price × limit) and cost scales with the limit too, so priority ≈ price (in µ-lamports) × 1e6 / 1e6 + base-fee term/limit — i.e. priority ≈ CU price plus a small bonus from the 2 500-lamport unburned base fee divided by cost. Requesting fewer CUs mostly helps via the base-fee term and via fitting under per-account/block limits (Q6), not via a large rank jump; raising the CU price is the direct lever.
- "Immediately after a bundle" is not a priority-queue position in Agave. The ledger position of a non-bundle buy is decided by which worker thread records its entry next after the bundle's entry; the scheduler guarantees only that, among conflicting buys on the same curve, the highest-priority one is handed to the lock-holding thread first.
- Because scheduling runs before receiving in each controller tick and receives are non-blocking while leader, a packet that arrives mid-slot is typically picked up within one tick (sub-millisecond to a few ms) and is eligible for the next scheduling pass; the CostPacer then decides whether budget remains at that instant.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch `thread_aware_account_locks.rs` (moved; 404 on master and v3.1.8) to quote the exact rule for write-after-read conflicts across threads; relied on the forum summary.
- No documented randomness in the Agave scheduler; non-determinism comes from thread timing, not an RNG (inference from code structure; no source states it explicitly).
- `TARGET_NUM_TRANSACTIONS_PER_BATCH` value not found in the fetched files.

## Q3. Jito-Agave: where bundles land relative to normal transactions, what lands right behind a bundle, and whether our own one-tx bundle beats a priority-fee transaction

### Takeaway
In jito-solana, bundles are executed by a separate `BundleStage` thread that runs concurrently with the banking-stage workers on the same bank; bundles are processed FIFO as delivered by the block engine (tip ordering happens in the block engine's 50 ms auction, by tip per requested CU), and every account a bundle touches is pre-locked so that banking-stage transactions on those accounts get `AccountInUse` and are retried immediately after the bundle unlocks. Bundles therefore do not go "first in the block"; they are interleaved, and the first non-bundle buy on the same curve after the create bundle is the highest-priority conflicting buy that the greedy scheduler had queued at the moment the lock cleared.

### Cited Findings
- Jito docs: "Bundles are groups of transactions (max 5)", "guaranteed to execute in the order they are listed", "execute within the same slot (e.g. a bundle cannot cross slot boundaries)", "executed sequentially and atomically meaning all-or-nothing"; "Parallel auctions are run at 50ms ticks"; bundles whose locks intersect on an account run in one auction; "Bundle orderings within a single auction are prioritized in the auction based on requested tip/cus-requested efficiency"; "Jito submits the highest paying combination of bundles to the validator up to some CU limit"; minimum tip 1 000 lamports to one of 8 tip accounts; `sendTransaction` is a proxy with `skip_preflight=true`, `bundleOnly=true` sends "exclusively as a single transaction bundle", recommended 70/30 split priority fee/tip; tips "do not prioritize transactions for validators that are not Jito-Solana leaders"; rate limit 1 req/s/IP/region; regional endpoints include `frankfurt.mainnet.block-engine.jito.wtf`; the docs do **not** say bundles execute before other transactions — [docs.jito.wtf low-latency send](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)
- `bundle_stage.rs` (master): BundleStage runs on its own thread `solBundleStgTx`; `process_buffered_bundles` uses the same `DecisionMaker` (`Consume(bank)` → `consume_bundles`; `Forward` → bundles cleared, never forwarded; `Hold` → buffered); per bundle `bundle_account_locker.lock_bundle(&bundle.transactions, bank)` before entering a `BUNDLE_WINDOW_SIZE = 10` processing window, `unlock_bundle` after `process_bundle`; `MAX_BUNDLE_RETRY_DURATION = 40 ms`; `SLOT_BOUNDARY_CHECK_PERIOD = 10 ms`; storage capacity 2 000; `bank.is_complete()` → retryable; `AccountInUse`, cost-model throttling and commit errors are retryable, other errors destroy the bundle; a per-slot tip-program init/crank runs once per slot before bundles — [jito-solana bundle_stage.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/bundle_stage.rs)
- `bundle_storage.rs`: two `VecDeque`s, `unprocessed_bundles` and `cost_model_buffered_bundles`; "Ordering is plain arrival order" — push_back/pop_front; nothing sorts by tip; a bundle that exceeded the cost model is parked until the slot changes, then moved to the front; `MAX_PACKETS_PER_BUNDLE = 5`; bundles are not dropped at slot end — [jito-solana bundle_storage.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/bundle_stage/bundle_storage.rs)
- `bundle_account_locker.rs`: ref-counted `write_locks`/`read_locks` maps; "Handles pre-locking bundle accounts so that accounts bundles touch can be reserved ahead"; `account_locks()` is "used in BankingStage during TransactionBatch construction" so BankingStage does not lock accounts already held by the locker — [jito-solana bundle_account_locker.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/bundle_stage/bundle_account_locker.rs)
- Banking-stage `consumer.rs` (jito): before building a batch it snapshots `bundle_account_locker.account_locks()`; a tx is rejected with `TransactionError::AccountInUse` if it writes an account that bundles hold for read **or** write, or reads an account bundles hold for write; the snapshot is dropped before execution; `AccountInUse` is "locking failure due to vote conflict or jito - immediately retry" (`immediately_retryable: true`) — [jito-solana consumer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/banking_stage/consumer.rs)
- Anza measurement (Feb 2026): uniformly-sent TPU transactions landed most uniformly across the slot on "Jito Labs validators"; Frankendancer "revenue" mode placed TPU transactions "toward the end of slots, while Jito bundles fill roughly the first three-quarters"; Harmonic near the end of the slot; AgaveBAM "step-like patterns consistent with 50ms batching" — [Chainflow summary of Anza](https://chainflow.io/lifetime-of-a-solana-transaction-from-sender-to-confirmation/), [Anza](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/transaction-landing-on-tpu)
- Third-party guides describe bundles as processed only by Jito-Solana leaders, forwarded by the block engine "to the next Jito-Solana leader", and selected by tip ("a bundle that tips below the going rate may never land") — [QuickNode Lil' JIT](https://www.quicknode.com/docs/solana/lil-jit-jito-bundles); bundles skip the normal forwarding path — same source
- Under BAM Direct Bundle Ingest (Oct 13 2026) bundles bypass the block engine's "roughly 50ms" hop and are ranked "by total fees (priority fees plus Jito tips) relative to requested compute"; block-engine `sendBundle`/`sendTransaction` traffic is now "directly forwarded to BAM through DBI, forgoing the Block engine's 50ms auction"; measured end-to-end bundle land rate 0.45 % → 0.57 %; average tip per landed bundle 0.000134 → 0.000231 SOL — [Solana Compass on Jito DBI](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop)

### Inferences
- Position k+1 after the create bundle on a Jito-Agave (non-BAM) leader: the bundle's entry is recorded by the BundleStage thread; the next entry in the ledger is whatever any banking worker records next. A competing buy on the same bonding curve cannot be in a batch built while the bundle holds the lock (it gets `AccountInUse`) and is retried immediately; so the first post-bundle buy is the highest-priority conflicting buy that was already in the queue (and assigned to the lock-holding thread) when the bundle's unlock happened. Arrival time matters only insofar as the buy must already be buffered when the lock clears; priority (CU price) decides among those present.
- A one-tx bundle from us (with tip) competes in a **different queue**: it is pre-locked and executed by BundleStage in block-engine arrival order. If the block engine delivers it after the create bundle and before other bundles, BundleStage executes it right behind the create bundle (window of 10, processed front-first, synchronously) — potentially ahead of any banking-stage buy, because the banking stage is blocked on the same accounts while our bundle holds them. The risk is the opposite ordering: if our bundle is delivered before the create executes, its buy fails (uninitialized curve) → non-retryable → destroyed. The 50 ms block-engine auction tick (or the DBI path under BAM) adds delay relative to a direct TPU packet. Sending both (TPU priority tx + bundle-of-one) covers both queues; the bundle path cannot be used for the "spray many leaders" trick since bundles are not forwarded.
- Because `MAX_BUNDLE_RETRY_DURATION` is 40 ms and the slot is 200 ms, a bundle that hits `AccountInUse` (e.g. because a banking-stage batch on the curve is in flight) retries within the slot rather than being lost.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how often a non-bundle TPU transaction lands immediately after a bundle on Jito-Agave leaders; the Anza placement data is at slot-fraction granularity, not entry adjacency.
- Whether the block engine (pre-DBI) forwarded a bundle only to the current leader or also to the next leader, and how many ms before the slot it delivered, is undocumented.
- The block engine's "up to some CU limit" for bundles per slot is not published.

## Q4. Firedancer / Frankendancer: how pack orders, how it treats bundles, and how late-arriving transactions are included

### Takeaway
Firedancer's pack tile keeps randomized treaps ordered by reward/compute (with a data-allocation penalty), schedules per microblock: votes first, then a bundle (FIFO, only when no votes were scheduled and in "balanced" mode only on bank tile 0), then normal transactions; conflicting writers go to per-account penalty treaps and are released when the account frees; a transaction arriving mid-slot is inserted immediately and is eligible at the next scheduling call that finds an idle bank tile. Frankendancer is being retired: it is unsupported through Alpenglow and Firedancer-family validators held ~15.8 % of top-500 stake on Oct 13 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Pack docs: comparator "Returns 1 if x.rewards/x.compute < y.rewards/y.compute"; treaps: pending, votes, bundles, and one penalty treap per conflicting account; conflict test `rw_bitset & in_use_bitset`; `max_write_cost_per_acct` exceeded → penalty treap; `FD_PACK_MAX_TXN_PER_BUNDLE = 5`; "Initializer bundles must succeed before normal bundles can be scheduled"; up to 62 execution tiles; written-account list cap 16 384 — [Firedancer block-packing internals (mirror)](https://mintlify.wiki/firedancer-io/firedancer/internals/block-packing)
- `fd_pack.c`: "bundles take priority over non-bundles, and earlier bundles take priority over later bundles"; `PENALTY_TREAP_THRESHOLD = 64` ("How many references to an account do we allow before subsequent transactions that write to the account go to the penalty treap"); `FD_PACK_SKIP_CNT = 50`; reward divisor `1 + (allocated_data × max_cost_per_block)/(cost_estimate × max_allocated_data_per_block)`; schedule order in `fd_pack_schedule_next_microblock`: votes (if `FD_PACK_SCHEDULE_VOTE`), then bundles "only ... if we didn't get any votes", then normal txns; a bundle with conflicts returns an empty microblock for that call; bundles check writable accounts "must not be in use at all" on other banks; normal txns on other bank tiles run concurrently if non-conflicting; when an account's in-use count hits zero the best penalty-treap candidate for it moves to pending; a tx that would exceed `max_write_cost_per_acct` gets `conflicts = ULONG_MAX` — "Can't be scheduled until the next block"; `fd_pack_end_block` clears in-use sets and resets initializer-bundle state — [firedancer fd_pack.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/disco/pack/fd_pack.c)
- `fd_pack_tile.c`: `CUS_PER_MICROBLOCK = 1 600 000`; `VOTE_FRACTION = 1.0` ("schedule all available votes first"); `MICROBLOCK_DURATION_NS = 0` (pacing gate off); strategy BALANCED (1): votes always allowed, bundles only to execle index 0, normal txns only to the first `pacing_execle_cnt` idle tiles (count derived from `fd_pack_pacing_*` from slot start/end ticks and `slot_max_cost`); PERF (0): every tile gets VOTE|BUNDLE|TXN; "they get scheduled in FIFO order" (bundles not paced); `compute_dynamic_max_microblocks` is "Monotonically decreasing over the slot lifetime" and uses "about 1us" per tx; when `now >= slot_end_ns` the block ends (reason TIME) and pack drains; a tx arriving during a slot is inserted in `after_frag` and becomes eligible on the next `after_credit` with an idle tile; `TRANSACTION_LIFETIME_SLOTS = 151` — [firedancer fd_pack_tile.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/disco/pack/fd_pack_tile.c)
- `default.toml`: `max_pending_transactions = 65524` (lowest estimated profitability dropped first); `use_consumed_cus = true` (unused CUs of over-requesting txs are given back to the block); `schedule_strategy = "balanced"` — "Try to fill the block at a rate that is just fast enough to fill it by the end ... optimizes for revenue from priority fees ... can be poor at capturing MEV if using an external block builder"; "perf" fills "as fast as possible" — [firedancer default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/app/fdctl/config/default.toml)
- Forum summary: deprecated "bundle" mode scheduled single transactions "only in the final 50 ms of a slot"; bundle treap limited to half the pack depth; eviction by reward/cost × scale factor — [Solana forum](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803)
- Firedancer team, Accelerate 2025 "Goldilocks rules": "Don't pack too fast: Slower execution allows better sampling of incoming transactions, which may increase fee capture"; "Don't pack too slowly"; dynamic thread count by position in the slot and remaining CU budget — [Solana Compass talk notes](https://solanacompass.com/learn/accelerate-25/scale-or-die-at-accelerate-2025-dropped-transactions-empty-blocks-michael-philip-firedancer)
- Measured: Frankendancer "revenue" mode puts TPU txns toward the end of the slot with bundles in the first ~3/4; Frankendancer was second-largest loss source — [Anza via Chainflow](https://chainflow.io/lifetime-of-a-solana-transaction-from-sender-to-confirmation/)
- Status: "Firedancer and Frankendancer are not supported during the migration" to Alpenglow; Firedancer-family stake among top-500 validators on Oct 13 2026 ≈ 15.8 % (Firedancer ~6.8 %, HarmonicFiredancer ~7.1 %, FireBAM ~1.6 %, Frankendancer ~0.3 %) — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc); Jump ends Frankendancer support at Alpenglow; full Firedancer live since Dec 2025 — [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jump-firedancer-ends-frankendancer-support/)

### Inferences
- On a balanced-mode Firedancer leader, a late-arriving high-priority buy is inserted at once and will be picked by the very next scheduling pass if a paced tile is idle; the "balanced" pacing deliberately leaves CU budget for late high-fee arrivals, so late arrival is less penalised than on an Agave leader whose CostPacer has already released the budget. Conversely, the bundle (create) executes on tile 0 while normal buys for the same curve are parked in the curve's penalty treap and released exactly when the bundle's microblock completes — the highest reward/compute buy in that treap goes first.
- Firedancer-family leaders will be absent from the leader set around Oct 21 and Frankendancer disappears entirely, so for late October 2026 the client mix to model is Agave / Jito-Agave / Jito-Agave-with-BAM / Harmonic.

### Gaps
- The exact `max_write_cost_per_acct`, `max_cost_per_block` values Firedancer uses at 200 ms slots were not found (they come from runtime limits, see Q6).
- No published measurement of Firedancer intra-slot placement separate from Frankendancer "revenue" mode.

## Q5. BAM: how BAM nodes order, plugins, what a trader must do differently, stake share in October 2026

### Takeaway
On a BAM-connected leader the validator no longer orders anything: a BAM node (TEE, AMD SEV-SNP) runs a periodic auction (~50 ms rounds per the Maker Plugin docs) in which "bundles and regular transactions compete on the same priority score", DBI ranks bundles "by total fees (priority fees plus Jito tips) relative to requested compute", and the validator executes the signed sequence FIFO with account-aware locking; plugins (Maker Priority Plugin live since Q2 2026) can pin specific transactions to the top of each batch regardless of fees. Transactions sent to a BAM validator's TPU port are routed through BAM anyway; direct submission to BAM nodes is whitelist-only. BAM-connected validators held 37 % of stake at epoch 1050 (Oct 9 2026), up from 34.1 % on Sep 9.

### Cited Findings
- BAM overview docs: BAM Node "ingests transactions and bundles, cluster state, and leader state ... validates and sequences them, sends them to the leader BAM Validator"; one node serves many validators, a validator connects to one node; the Block Engine supplies packet and pre-simulated bundle streams to the node for backward compatibility; node scheduler must "Sequence transactions by programmatic, stable rules"; validators "must execute all received transactions in the exact order they receive them" or be disconnected; "If a validator is connected to BAM and is scheduled to lead soon, transactions submitted via RPC are routed through BAM"; "Transactions sent to a validator's TPU port are processed through BAM before execution"; validator enables with `--bam-url`; "BAM sequences both normal transactions and Jito Bundles" — [bam.dev docs overview](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/bam-overview/)
- Maker Plugin: "Every auction round (~50 ms), BAM assembles a batch of transactions for the validator in two sequential phases"; Phase 1 puts each enrolled maker's latest update at the front, which "cannot be changed by paying higher fees or tips"; Phase 2: "bundles and regular transactions compete on the same priority score under the same scheduling logic"; enrolment is static, operator-configured, requires a CU-price floor and a seqno; "No Jito tip required" for makers — [bam.dev Maker Plugin how it works](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/maker-plugin/how-it-works/)
- Helius BAM explainer (Jito design claims): transactions "enter a mempool and take part in a frequent periodic auction"; the first scheduler "subdivides each block into N slots and allocates CUs equally across intra-block auctions"; validator client has "a FIFO executor that uses account-aware locking"; "Users can send transactions directly to BAM instances to avoid validators seeing packets"; "If disconnected from a BAM node, validators fall back to their own TPU"; plugins = Application-Controlled Execution; planned plugins: cancel-before-take, JIT oracle updates (Pyth), blockspace futures, time-in-force, preconfirmations; codebase closed-source; TEE = AMD SEV-SNP with 2–5 % overhead — [Helius: BAM](https://www.helius.dev/blog/block-assembly-marketplace-bam)
- jito-solana `bam_scheduler.rs`: "A Scheduler implementation that pulls batches off the container, and then schedules them to workers in a FIFO, account-aware manner"; each BAM batch (≤5 txs) is a `PrioGraph` node; conflicting later batches wait for earlier ones, non-conflicting later batches may execute first and results can return out of order; `NUM_BAM_WORKERS = 8` outstanding batches; `try_admit_transactions` reserves block cost in pop order and defers if the shortfall is covered by in-flight estimates; at a slot boundary unscheduled batches are returned with `OutsideLeaderSlot`; `revert_on_error` batches are atomic — [jito-solana bam_scheduler.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/bam_scheduler.rs)
- Direct Bundle Ingest (BAM Team post, 13 Oct 2026): searchers "submit a bundle directly to a BAM node over QUIC or JSON-RPC"; node verifies signatures, simulates, drops failing bundles; survivors "enter BAM's auction, ranked by total fees (priority fees plus Jito tips) relative to requested compute"; winners go to the validator "as a single batch"; access is by whitelist via a form; "BAM-connected validators hold 164 million SOL, 37% of Solana stake as of epoch 1050"; all BAM nodes enter Maintenance Mode before Alpenglow activation (DBI unavailable) — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop)
- Preconfirmations (live Sep 9 2026): stream of transactions the leader "committed to executing", emitted after scheduling and "before those transactions are shredded"; p50 5–10 ms ahead of shred streams; distributed via Helius and Triton; "says what will execute if the block lands, not that the block will land" — [bam.dev preconfirmations](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/preconfirmations/); at launch BAM validators = 34.1 % of stake across 383 of 665 validators (12 % in Jan 2026); validators join with `--bam-url`, Firedancer validators via FireBAM; a BAM Verifier disconnects validators whose blocks deviate from commitments — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-bam-preconfirmations-go-live-on-solana-covering-34-of-network-stake)
- Earlier share points: 27.7 % (Q1 2026 end, Blockworks), ~33 % at Q2 2026 end with 378 validators — [Solana Compass Q2](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-q2-2026-protocol-revenue-falls-45-to-128m-as-bam-reaches-33-of-solana-stake)
- Anza measured AgaveBAM leaders as the largest source of lost transactions ("isolated drops") and as showing 50 ms step-like placement — [Anza via Chainflow](https://chainflow.io/lifetime-of-a-solana-transaction-from-sender-to-confirmation/)

### Inferences
- For a sniper on a BAM leader (≈37 % of leader slots): there is no way to be "right behind" a bundle by timing — ordering within a ~50 ms round is by a fee-per-requested-CU score shared by bundles and plain transactions, and the create bundle and our buy are only adjacent if they fall in the same round and ours is the next-highest score touching that curve. A TPU-sent transaction is fine (it is routed into BAM), but anything that reaches the node after the round closes waits for the next ~50 ms round; at 200 ms slots that is up to a quarter of the slot. A whitelisted DBI connection removes the block-engine hop (~50 ms) for bundles; for plain transactions the fastest path is still the leader's TPU (or RPC → BAM).
- Priority score under BAM counts the Jito tip as fee, so on BAM leaders a tip is a direct ordering lever for plain transactions too (bundle-of-one not required), whereas on non-BAM Jito-Agave leaders a tip buys nothing for a non-bundle transaction.
- The Maker Priority Plugin only affects enrolled market-maker updates on enrolled markets; no plugin was found that touches pump.fun or memecoin launches.

### Gaps
- No primary BAM document states the exact auction cadence for ordinary transactions (the ~50 ms figure is from the Maker Plugin page; the Helius article says "frequent periodic"), nor the tie-break rule within a round.
- No October-2026 stake figure after epoch 1050 (37 %) was found; the "which share runs BAM in October 2026" answer is 37 % as of Oct 9.
- Whether BAM nodes accept unstaked QUIC connections for plain transactions and how they weight them (SWQoS equivalent) is undocumented.

## Q6. Account write-lock contention and per-account/per-block compute limits (pump.fun bonding curve as a hot account)

### Takeaway
Ten buys on the same bonding curve are serialised by write-lock: in Agave the greedy scheduler hands conflicting transactions to the thread that holds the lock in priority order (others wait or are re-queued), in Firedancer they sit in a per-account penalty treap released as the account frees. The per-account write limit is 40 % of the block limit under SIMD-0306 (24 M CU on Agave master, i.e. 40 % of 60 M; news coverage says per-slot limits were scaled down for 200 ms slots to 50 M CU per block, which would imply ~20 M per account) — far above what a few dozen ~100 k-CU buys consume, so it does not cut off late buys on launch slots; the block limit and pacing matter more.

### Cited Findings
- Agave master `block_cost_limits.rs`: `MAX_BLOCK_UNITS = MAX_BLOCK_UNITS_SIMD_0256 = 60_000_000`; `MAX_BLOCK_UNITS_SIMD_0286 = 100_000_000` behind `simd_0286_block_limit()`; `MAX_WRITABLE_ACCOUNT_UNITS = 24_000_000`; `MAX_BLOCK_ACCOUNTS_DATA_SIZE_DELTA = 100_000_000`; `WRITE_LOCK_UNITS = 300`; `SIGNATURE_COST = 720`; no slot-time-scaled variants in this file — [agave block_cost_limits.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/cost-model/src/block_cost_limits.rs)
- SIMD-0306 ("Raise Account CU Limits"): per-account limit moves from fixed 12 M to 40 % of the block limit, "initially rise from 12 million to 24 million CUs ... eventually to 40 million CUs once SIMD-0286 (100M CU blocks) is activated", to be activated in the Agave 3.0 cycle — [Helius Agave 3.0](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v3-0)
- 100 M CU blocks (SIMD-0286) live on mainnet July 29 2026 (epoch 1009); the page says the writable-account limit "(12M CUs) ... stayed the same" — conflicts with the 24 M constant above — [solana.com 100M CU blocks](https://solana.com/upgrades/100m-cu-blocks)
- SIMD-0525 scales per-slot limits with slot time; news table: 100 M at 400 ms → 87.5 M (350) → 75 M (300) → 62.5 M (250) → 50 M (200 ms), with account-write, vote, data-allocation and shred budgets "all reduced under the proposal" (values not given) — [egamers summary of SIMD-0525](https://egamers.io/?p=103358); Solana Compass: "SIMD-0525 scales per-slot limits in proportion to slot time, so each block has half the room it had at 400ms" — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solanas-200ms-slot-time-is-live-on-mainnet-at-epoch-1053-completing-simd-0525)
- Agave conflict handling: a tx needing locks held by a thread is placed on that thread; otherwise any thread; blocked high-priority txs hold back lower-priority conflicting ones in the prio-graph scheduler, while the greedy scheduler "scans past" a blocked tx and schedules the next schedulable one — [Solana forum](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803), [agave greedy_scheduler.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/greedy_scheduler.rs)
- Agave SIMD-0083 (3.0): conflicting transactions may share an entry and "run sequentially in the order they appear in the entry" — [Helius Agave 3.0](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v3-0)
- Firedancer: >64 references to one account send further writers to that account's penalty treap; a tx exceeding `max_write_cost_per_acct` "Can't be scheduled until the next block"; released when the account's in-use count hits zero — [fd_pack.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/disco/pack/fd_pack.c)
- Jito-Agave: banking-stage txs that write an account a bundle holds (read or write) get `AccountInUse` and are immediately retryable — [jito-solana consumer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/banking_stage/consumer.rs)
- Helius: "Smaller CU requests make a transaction more likely to be included"; over-requesting "makes scheduling harder because the scheduler can't know remaining block capacity before execution" — [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/how-to-land-transactions-on-solana)
- Firedancer `use_consumed_cus = true` returns unused CUs of over-requesting transactions to the block budget — [default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/app/fdctl/config/default.toml)

### Inferences
- Ordering among same-curve buys on Agave/Jito-Agave is priority order (CU price-dominated) **among the buys present in the queue when the lock frees**; a buy that arrives 5 ms later than a lower-priced one but before the lock frees still wins. Requested CU limit matters here mainly through cost reservation: Agave reserves the requested limit against block and per-account limits at scheduling time (consumer "QosService::compute_transaction_costs"; Firedancer refunds unused CUs), so a 1.4 M-CU request for a 100 k-CU buy would eat per-account budget and make it harder to fit at slot end.
- The per-account cap (≥20 M CU even on the conservative reading) would require ~200 buys of 100 k CU each on one curve within one 200 ms block before cutting anyone off; this is not the binding constraint for pump.fun launches. Block CU pacing (Q7) and the raw 200 ms time budget are.

### Gaps
- The actual mainnet per-account and per-block CU limits at 200 ms slots could not be pinned to a primary source: Agave master shows 60 M/100 M and 24 M constants with no slot scaling, the Foundation page says 12 M unchanged in July 2026, and news says SIMD-0525 scaled limits to 50 M per block. The SIMD-0525 and SIMD-0306 texts were not fetched.
- No empirical study of pump.fun bonding-curve lock contention or of how many same-curve buys land per launch slot was found.

## Q7. How late in the slot can a transaction arrive and still be included, per client, and the banking-stage deadlines

### Takeaway
No client publishes a cut-off; the practical rule set is: Agave paces the block budget linearly over `slot − 50 ms` and admits anything the budget allows until the bank completes (PoH tick height / Alpenglow clock), with a non-blocking receive every scheduler tick; Firedancer ends the block at `slot_end_ns` and drains, with "balanced" pacing deliberately keeping budget for late arrivals; Jito bundles retry for up to 40 ms; BAM batches in ~50 ms rounds and returns anything unscheduled at the slot boundary. Anza's Feb 2026 measurements show the empirical pattern: Jito-Agave leaders place uniformly through the slot, Frankendancer-revenue and Harmonic push TPU transactions to the slot end, AgaveBAM shows 50 ms steps, and transactions in flight across a leader-window boundary "may be lost".

### Cited Findings
- Agave `CostPacer`: `allocation_per_milli = block_limit / fill_time`; `DEFAULT_SCHEDULER_PACING_NON_FILL_TIME_MILLIS = 50`; fill time defaults to `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT − 50` and is clamped with a warning if it exceeds the slot time; after fill time elapses the full remaining `block_limit` is schedulable; budget is reduced by `shared_block_cost` already used (e.g. by bundles) — [agave scheduler_controller.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/scheduler_controller.rs); SDK `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT` = 300 ms on master while the cluster runs 200 ms — [solana-sdk clock](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/solana-sdk/master/clock/src/lib.rs)
- Agave receive: while leader, `try_recv` up to 512 packets per tick, no blocking; scheduling precedes receiving within a tick — [receive_and_buffer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/receive_and_buffer.rs), [scheduler_controller.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/scheduler_controller.rs)
- Jito BundleStage: `MAX_BUNDLE_RETRY_DURATION = 40 ms`, `SLOT_BOUNDARY_CHECK_PERIOD = 10 ms`, receive timeout 0 ms when bundles are buffered else 10 ms; `bank.is_complete()` → retryable (parked to next slot) — [bundle_stage.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/bundle_stage.rs)
- Firedancer: block ends when `now >= slot_end_ns` (reason TIME) then drains until all tiles idle; microblock bound decreases monotonically over the slot; balanced mode paces normal txns via `fd_pack_pacing_enabled_bank_cnt`; a tx inserted mid-slot is eligible at the next `after_credit` with an idle tile — [fd_pack_tile.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/disco/pack/fd_pack_tile.c); "balanced" = "fill the block at a rate that is just fast enough to fill it by the end" — [default.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/app/fdctl/config/default.toml)
- BAM: ~50 ms auction rounds (Maker Plugin page); validator-side scheduler returns unscheduled batches with `OutsideLeaderSlot` at the slot boundary; outstanding work "finishes against the old configuration" — [bam.dev](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/maker-plugin/how-it-works/), [bam_scheduler.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/bam_scheduler.rs)
- Measured placement (Anza, Feb 2026, 400 ms slots): uniform-in-time sends land non-uniformly; Jito Labs most uniform; Frankendancer revenue mode and Harmonic produce "spikes near slot boundaries"; spikes just after a boundary "likely reflect transactions received in the previous slot and carried over"; transactions sent near the end of a leader window "risk being in flight during a leader transition and may be lost"; ~3 % of lost-tx target blocks were vote-only — [Anza](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/transaction-landing-on-tpu)
- Slot now 200 ms; measured average at the 250 ms stage was 266–269 ms (15–19 ms over target); no 200 ms average published yet as of Oct 9 — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solanas-200ms-slot-time-is-live-on-mainnet-at-epoch-1053-completing-simd-0525)
- Alpenglow (epoch 1064, Oct 21 ~14:50 UTC): migration = feature gate at epoch boundary → 5 000 slots later blocks become vote-only → genesis certificate at 82 % stake → rollback and handoff ("hundreds of milliseconds") → user transactions resume; required clients Agave / Jito-Solana / Harmonic Salsa on Agave 4.3.0; Firedancer/Frankendancer unsupported; PoH no longer paces blocks, `Clock` set by the leader within bounds; finality target ~150 ms; 0.8 SOL per-epoch Validator Admission Ticket — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc); leader window stays 4 slots; votes leave blockspace, "relieve load on the transaction processing unit (TPU) and replay"; next leader "can start transmitting the next block" as soon as it ingests the parent; Rotor is a later SIMD — [Helius Alpenglow](https://www.helius.dev/blog/alpenglow)

### Inferences
- On an Agave leader at 200 ms, the pacer releases ~1/150 of the block budget per ms (if fill time clamps to 150 ms = 200 − 50) and everything remaining in the last 50 ms; a buy arriving at t = 150 ms+ competes only against whatever is still queued, with full budget — late arrival is penalised by time-to-bank-completion, not by budget. Our packet must therefore be at the leader before the bank completes; given Anza's 0.25–0.29 slot mean latency from Amsterdam at 400 ms (≈100–115 ms wall clock), a Frankfurt bot reacting to a bundle observed via shreds (which themselves lag the leader by tens of ms) will in most cases **miss the creation slot entirely** at 200 ms slots and compete in the next slot of the same leader window instead; being first in that slot then follows the Q2/Q3 rules.
- The only way to be in the same slot as the create is to not depend on seeing it on-chain: pre-position (be already queued at the leader before the create executes, so the buy is the first conflicting tx released when the lock clears) or receive a pre-execution signal (BAM preconfirmations give 5–10 ms over shreds on BAM leaders only).
- After Alpenglow, bank completion is clock-driven rather than PoH-tick-driven and votes leave the block, which slightly increases usable CU/time for user transactions; nothing in the published material changes the scheduler, SWQoS or fee rules.

### Gaps
- No source gives a measured "latest arrival offset inside the slot that still lands" for any client; only slot-fraction placement histograms exist (Anza), and those predate 200 ms slots.
- The effective Agave fill time at 200 ms (whether operators run a 150 ms fill or the SDK default clamps) is not documented.
- Post-Alpenglow behaviour of the Jito BundleStage and BAM (Jito's own minimum version was unannounced as of Oct 9) is unknown; BAM nodes will be in maintenance mode around activation.
