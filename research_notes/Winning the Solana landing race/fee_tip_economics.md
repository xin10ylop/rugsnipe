# Fee, tip and compute-unit economics of pump.fun creation-slot snipers (as of 2026-10-18)

Scope note. Public, pump.fun-specific distributions of creation-slot fees do not exist as a Dune/Flipside dashboard or a published paper (searched; see Gaps). The only creation-slot-level numbers found are (a) this project's own on-chain scans in `docs/research/gap-2.md` and `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` (Oct 2026, unpublished, labelled "internal" below), (b) Jito's network-wide tip-floor API and BAM statistics, and (c) vendor claims. Measurements and claims are separated in each section. The arXiv paper closest to the topic (2607.02795, "Coordinated Sniper Cohorts on Pump.fun") withdrew all of its numbers in an October 2026 correction and does not report fees or slot counts anyway — [arXiv:2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795).

## KQ1. What do winning creation-slot buyers pay today (priority fee, total fee, Jito tip)?

### Takeaway
On contested pump.fun launches in October 2026 the first reactive winner's median total bid (priority fee + sender/Jito tip) is about 0.003–0.004 SOL, p75 about 0.014–0.017 SOL, p90 about 0.05–0.07 SOL; the generic Jito tip floor on the same day is two to three orders of magnitude lower (p50 ≈ 7,760 lamports, p99 ≈ 0.001 SOL), so the pump.fun creation slot is a distinct, much more expensive fee market than "Solana at large".

### Cited Findings
Measurements
- Live Jito tip floor, 2026-10-18 16:27 UTC (all landed bundles, network-wide): p25 2,979 lamports; p50 7,760 lamports; p75 10,630 lamports; p95 150,000 lamports (0.00015 SOL); p99 1,022,028 lamports (0.00102 SOL); EMA of p50 6,871 lamports — [Jito tip floor API](https://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_floor) (fetched directly). The API documents these fields and a WebSocket tip stream; the Jito dashboard is at explorer.jito.wtf/tip-floor — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/).
- A second reading of the same API on an earlier day in Oct 2026 gave p50 6.9e-6, p75 1e-5, p95 3.6e-4, p99 8.7e-4 SOL — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` (Oct 2026).
- Jito's own before/after BAM-DBI comparison (3–20 Sept vs 20 Sept–7 Oct 2026): average tip per landed bundle rose from 0.000134 SOL to 0.000231 SOL (+72%); tips on landed bundles 14,279 SOL → 28,765 SOL; bundles received 23.76B → 21.97B; bundles landed 106.3M → 124.3M; end-to-end land rate 0.45% → 0.57% — [Solana Compass on Jito's DBI post (13 Oct 2026)](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop).
- `getRecentPrioritizationFees` for the pump.fun program on 2026-10-18 (public mainnet RPC, 150 slots): most slots report 0 µL/CU as the per-slot minimum among landed transactions referencing the program, with isolated slots at 1,199,986; 1,643,832; 1,999,684; 4,431,854; 4,798,433 and 5,294,272 µL/CU — fetched directly from `api.mainnet-beta.solana.com` (the method returns per-slot floors, not fees paid; see Chainstack's caveat that averaging it gives "the average or median *floor*, not the average or median fee actually paid" — [Chainstack](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-estimate-priority-fees-getrecentprioritizationfees)).
- Internal scan of ~3,000 SOL-quoted pump.fun creates with 7,073 blocks fetched (`getBlock`, slot 0 and slot +1), Oct 2026: for the **first slot-0 reactive winner** the median total bid (priority fee + tips) is **0.0034 SOL, p75 0.0165, p90 0.068**; across **all slot-0 winners** total bid p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 = 0.00002 / 0.0002 / **0.0018** / 0.0089 / 0.039 SOL; priority fee only, winners: 0.000001 / 0.000045 / 0.00079 / 0.004 / 0.0177 SOL; priority fee only, slippage losers: 0.000004 / 0.000031 / 0.00015 / 0.0028 / 0.0125 SOL — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4.
- Same scan: in contested launches (≥2 slot-0 winners, n=327) the first winner's median bid is 0.006 SOL against 0.0011 SOL for later winners — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4.
- Internal 87-create study (slots 456,764,000–456,765,819, about 6.5 minutes, Oct 2026): reactive block-0 **winners paid p25 0.0015, p50 0.004, p75 0.0138, p90 0.049 SOL** (priority fee + tips as bid); losers p50 0.0012 SOL; largest seen a 0.15 SOL Helius tip and a 0.256 SOL priority fee — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Same study, compute: reactive block-0 winners consumed p50 59k CU for the whole transaction (ATA create + compute budget + tip + buy) with a requested limit of p50 75k; `buy_v3` alone p10/p50 = 49.5k/51.2k CU, legacy `buy` p50 117k — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Same study, bid ladder expressed in µL/CU at a 62k CU limit: "typical block-0 winner (observed p50)" ≈ 16M µL/CU (0.001 SOL priority) + 0.003 SOL tip ≈ 0.004 SOL; "competitive (p75)" ≈ 60M µL/CU (0.0037 SOL) + 0.01 tip ≈ 0.014 SOL; "hardcore (p90)" ≈ 160M µL/CU (0.0099 SOL) + 0.039 tip ≈ 0.05 SOL; floor with no rivals (Helius `swqos_only`, 100k µL/CU) ≈ 0.000016 SOL — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §5.
- Bitquery (fee anatomy, undated): failed transactions pay a higher average fee than successful ones because priority fees are highest where competition is fiercest — [Bitquery](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Solana/solana-fee-anatomy/).
- Solscan's Fee Tracker charts per-block fee percentiles (P25/P50/P75) and splits non-vote cost into base fees, priority fees and Jito tips; it is network-wide, not pump.fun-specific — [Solscan](https://info.solscan.io/solscan-fee-tracker-dashboard).
- QuickNode's Solana gas tracker snapshot (undated, no method): "Low/Medium/High" averages 61,328 / 140,000 / 281,174 µL/CU — [QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/gas-tracker/solana).

Claims (vendors, guides; unverified and mutually inconsistent)
- One guide estimates 0.002 SOL priority + 0.005 SOL Jito tip per 1 SOL buy and says 0.002–0.005 SOL is needed "to be in the running" for launches with bot competition; another claims priority fees alone of 0.05–0.5 SOL per attempted snipe (a ~100× gap) — [RPC Fast guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump); [memegateway](https://memegateway.com/academy/solana-trading-bot-fees-compared-2026/).
- Bundler vendors: tips "typically run between 0.0001 and 0.005 SOL"; another says 0.001–0.01 SOL normally and up to 0.05 SOL at peak congestion — [solbundler](https://solbundler.app/blog/how-to-bundle-pump-fun); [scorplabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-bundle-detection).
- RPC Fast: migration tip floors "can rise 10–50x in the seconds around the event", so hardcoded tips fail; tips should be set from the last 50 blocks of tip data times a coefficient tied to bonding-curve velocity (no SOL numbers given; article dated 29 May 2026, updated 7 Sept 2026) — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump).
- Dysnix (Feb 2026, updated Sept 2026): "Jito priority routing" on some platforms ≈ 0.006 SOL per transaction; individual snipe tx cost "fractions of a cent to several cents"; effective cost per successful trade $2.65 (production) to $7.75 (amateur), with $4.80 of failed-attempt overhead per successful trade for amateurs — [Dysnix](https://dysnix.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot) (vendor-authored; figures from partner RPC Fast).
- Chainstack/RPC Fast explainers (Jun–Oct 2026): "Jito tips account for more than 60% of Solana's priority-fee volume" by mid-2026 — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana); Pine Analytics put Jito at >60% of priority-fee volume in early 2025 — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jitos-role-in-solana-deep-dive).

### Inferences
- The gap between the generic tip floor (p50 ≈ 0.0000078 SOL) and the creation-slot winner's bid (p50 ≈ 0.003–0.004 SOL, ~400× higher) means the Jito `tip_floor` API is useless as a sizing signal for pump.fun creation slots; it is a lower bound only. Sizing has to come from a per-launch history of creation-slot bids (the internal data), not from network percentiles.
- Because most of the bid is tip rather than priority fee (internal p50 priority-only 0.00079 SOL vs total 0.0018 SOL), and tips revert on failure (KQ3), the effective at-risk cost per failed attempt is dominated by the priority fee leg.
- The internal p50/p75/p90 ladder (0.004 / 0.014 / 0.05 SOL) suggests a flat 0.05 SOL tip (TradeWiz-style, per the internal note) overpays on roughly 90% of launches.

### Gaps
- No Dune or Flipside dashboard with per-launch creation-slot fee/tip distributions was found; the Solscan Fee Tracker and Jito tip floor are network-wide only.
- No peer-reviewed or research-firm distribution of creation-slot µL/CU exists; the only µL/CU figures are the internal ladder (derived) and the per-slot floors from `getRecentPrioritizationFees`.
- The internal numbers are from one week of October 2026 and have not been published or independently replicated.

## KQ2. How many buys land in the creation slot of a bundled launch, and how is their order explained?

### Takeaway
Measured in Oct 2026, only about 22% of SOL-quoted creates draw even one independent reactive winner in slot 0 (6.4% draw three or more), but on bundled, CEX-funded launches a median 3.4 independent wallets attempt slot 0 and about 1.6 win; order within the slot is explained first by arrival time and second by bid, with the earlier winner having bid more in ~73% of contested pairs.

### Cited Findings
Measurements (internal, Oct 2026, unpublished)
- 22.1% (±1.5) of SOL-quoted creates drew ≥1 independent reactive buyer that succeeded in slot 0; 6.4% drew ≥3; 2.1% drew ≥5. Counting slot 0 plus slot +1: 34.6%, 13.6%, 5.6%. Any non-creator slot-0 buy including the creator's own bundle: 31.5% — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §1–2.
- On CEX-fresh + bundled launches: median 3.4 independent wallets attempt slot 0, about 1.6 win, 85% are bought by someone by slot +1 — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §2.
- Random-launch sample (n=396): slot-0 wallet count p25/p50/p75/p90 = 1/1/2/5; bundled launches (n=107): 3/4/6/7; "interesting" bundled launches (ATH ≥ $20k, n=26): 4/6/13/18; 27% of random launches were bundled; 19.9% showed an adjacent-transaction (Jito-like) bundle — `data/reports/firstslot_tables.md`.
- 87-create study: 26% of creates drew any non-creator same-slot buy; of 85 block-0 buys, 24 sat at index gap 1–3 right after the create (treated as dev/launcher bundles; one confirmed as the same Jito bundle via `bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/transaction/<sig>`), 61 were reactive (gap ≥4): 33 succeeded, 28 failed, 27 of the failures on slippage — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Ordering: among launches with ≥2 slot-0 winners, the earlier winner had the higher total bid in 73.4% of pairs (n=1,892). The first winner's median index gap after the create is 149 transactions (≈13% of the block, ≈28 ms); the fastest repeat snipers land 5–9 transactions after the create — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4.
- For *all* pump trades (not just contested snipes), the earlier of two same-mint same-block trades had the higher fee/CU only 54.0% of the time on non-BAM Agave leaders (9,702 pairs), 58.3% on BAM leaders (5,938), and 56.6% per-(slot,mint) on Firedancer (10,308); pure FIFO would score 50%, strict priority 100% — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.
- Reaction time: converting index gap to time, the fastest third-party buys landed about 9–30 ms after the create executed; create position within its block p25/p50/p75 = 24%/48%/81%; creates in the last ~15% of a block almost never drew a block-0 buy — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Fill premium over the post-bundle price by reactive rank (median, p75): 1st +1.1%/+3.9%; 3rd +10.8%/+21.2%; 5th +15.8%/+35.1%; on CEX-fresh bundled launches the 3rd pays +15.9% and the 5th +32.6% (n=17) — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §5.

Published measurements and claims
- Pine Analytics (21 Apr 2025): "over 50% of tokens are now sniped in the exact block they're created" (no count given); 15,000+ tokens in one month sniped by a wallet that had exchanged SOL with the deployer; deployers "funded multiple wallets that all signed and sniped in the same transaction", splitting buys across 2–4 wallets; 87% of these snipes profitable; >55% fully exited in under a minute — [Pine Analytics, "Exit Liquidity Machines"](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines). The internal Oct 2026 scan finds 31.5% for any non-creator slot-0 buy and attributes the difference to Pine's figure being stale or including bundles — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §7.
- Jito bundles hold at most 5 transactions, execute sequentially and atomically, and cannot cross slot boundaries — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/). Vendors nonetheless advertise 16–20 wallet "block 0" buys using multiple bundle groups or multi-signer transactions — [solbundler](https://solbundler.app/blog/how-to-bundle-pump-fun).
- A detection heuristic used by bundle checkers: "three or more buys sharing the creation slot = bundle" — [scorplabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-bundle-detection).
- Dysnix (vendor, no method): "a typical Pump.fun-tier launch attracts 200+ competing bots in the first 500 ms"; illustrative funnel ~140 detect, ~90 pass pre-flight, ~50 reach leader TPU, ~25 land in first 10 confirmations, ~10 exit profitably — [Dysnix](https://dysnix.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot).
- Chorus One (16 Mar 2026), footrule distance D between fee-priority order and execution order: Agave leaders "arrival-dominated" with D peaking near 0.66 (close to random); BAM D ≈ 0.6 (priority reflected only within small batches); Firedancer/Frankendancer D ≈ 0.40–0.45, "clearly and consistently" priority-aligned; leader-level data 8–18 Dec 2025 — [Chorus One](https://chorus.one/reports-research/the-state-of-schedulers-on-solana-2).
- Nozomi: "with equal tips, the transaction that arrived first wins"; when transactions write the same account they compete in an auction and the tip determines position; several can still land in the same slot — [Nozomi docs](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq).
- arXiv 2607.02795 (June 2026 data, 166,098 launches) studied 2–12-wallet "cohorts" that co-fire as early buyers but reports no slot-level counts, and its numbers were withdrawn in v4 (Oct 2026) — [arXiv:2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795). The rug-pull paper 2603.24625 (100,063 tokens, H1 2025) has no creation-slot statistics — [arXiv:2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625).

### Inferences
- The creator's bundle is the only reliably "ordered" block of buys (adjacent indices, same Jito bundle); reactive buys are scattered later in the block, so the realistic target is "first reactive after the bundle", not "inside the bundle".
- Order among reactive winners is a two-stage process: arrival into the same scheduling batch (50 ms Jito/BAM/Harmonic tick, Agave batch) decides which batch you are in; the bid (tip on builder paths, fee/CU on Agave/Firedancer) only reorders within that batch. The 73% figure for contested snipes versus 54–58% for all trades is consistent with snipers clustering in the same batch.
- "200+ bots per launch" is not supported by on-chain data: the attempt counts that actually land are single digits, which means most competitors either filter launches, fail pre-flight, or never reach the leader.

### Gaps
- No public dataset gives the count of competing buyers per creation slot; all per-slot counts above are internal.
- Same-bundle membership of reactive buys could only be confirmed for one transaction (via Jito's bundle lookup API); the share of reactive buys that arrived as single-tx Jito bundles versus plain transactions is unknown beyond tip-account attribution.

## KQ3. What does a sniper pay when it loses the race?

### Takeaway
A transaction that lands but fails on slippage pays the 5,000-lamport base fee per signature plus the full priority fee computed on the *requested* CU limit (not consumed CU), and advances its durable nonce; the tip, if it is a transfer instruction inside the same transaction, reverts and is not paid; a transaction that never lands or fails validation costs nothing.

### Cited Findings
Protocol rules
- Total fee = base fee (5,000 lamports per signature, 50% burned) + priority fee (`ceil(cu_price × cu_limit / 1,000,000)`, 100% to validator). "If the transaction fails, the fee is still charged." Fee is "deducted from the fee payer before execution begins." "The priority fee is based on the requested CU limit, not actual CU usage." Default limit when none is set: 200,000 CU per non-builtin instruction, 3,000 per builtin — [Solana docs, fee structure](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/fee-structure.md).
- "Do failed transactions charge fees? Yes, because validators still process them." — [Solana learn](https://solana.com/learn/understanding-solana-transaction-fees.md).
- Durable nonces: if a nonce transaction "fails validation (nonce already used, authority not signed, account not found), the entire transaction is dropped. No fees collected, no state changes." If it "passes validation but execution fails," the nonce still advances and fees are still collected — [Solana docs, durable nonces](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/durable-nonces.md); same rule in the Anza implemented proposal, which explains that otherwise a failed transaction "could be replayed until the stored nonce is successfully advanced" — [Anza docs](https://docs.anza.xyz/implemented-proposals/durable-tx-nonces).
- A dropped transaction (never reached a leader/block) has "no on-chain record, no fee, no error log" — [RPC Fast error guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-transaction-error-code).
- Jito advises putting the tip in the same transaction as the strategy logic so that "if the transaction fails, you don't pay the Jito tip"; bundles "can only contain successful transactions" — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/).
- Nozomi: "You only pay when your transaction lands," because the tip is an instruction inside the transaction; if the transaction fails the tip is never charged; transactions below the 0.001 SOL minimum are silently dropped — [Nozomi docs](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq).
- Helius Sender: a priority fee set via `setComputeUnitPrice` is mandatory; examples use `skipPreflight: true`, `maxRetries: 0`, a 100,000 CU limit and 200,000 µL/CU; it does not state whether the tip is refunded on failure (the tip is a transfer in the same transaction) — [Helius Sender docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender).
- Jito `sendTransaction` always runs with skip_preflight and is forwarded as a bundle — internal note citing Jito docs, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.
- Agave scheduler priority (source): `P = R / (1 + C)` with a 1,000,000 multiplier, where R is the validator's reward (priority fee plus the unburned half of the base fee) and C is the cost-model cost of the transaction — [agave `core/src/transaction_priority.rs`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/transaction_priority.rs). The internal note adds that the default 64 MiB loaded-accounts-data-size limit adds 16,384 CU of "phantom cost" to C; tight-setting it to 12–16 MiB (≈3–4k CU) gives ≈20% more priority per lamport on a ~62k CU buy; at least 56% of pump buys in the scan never set it — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.

Measured failure economics (internal, Oct 2026)
- Per attempting wallet in slot 0: 46% won, 22% failed only on pump slippage (`6042 BuySlippageBelowMinTokensOut` ≫ `6002 TooMuchSolRequired`: 732 vs 121 txs), 29% were on-chain bot-guard aborts from wrapper programs, 3% other; on bundled repeat-creator launches slippage reaches 38%; in slot +1 slippage is 12.6% and guard aborts 54.8% — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §3.
- Slippage losers' priority fee (the only part they pay, since tips revert): p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 = 0.000004 / 0.000031 / 0.00015 / 0.0028 / 0.0125 SOL — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4.
- Bot-guard sprays (programs that fire at many creates and abort on-chain) have a median priority fee of 0.000005 SOL — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §3. All 360 System `0x0` failures came from wallets that had already won the same mint (multi-sender duplicates killed by a guard account), so per-transaction failure rates overstate losses — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §3.
- Slippage losers are not simply late: a winner sat at a lower index than a slippage loser in only 61.1% of same-launch pairs and had a higher priority fee in only 55.1% (n=3,537); most losers set `min_tokens_out`/`max_sol_cost` too tight against a price the bundle had already moved — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §3.
- 87-create study: 28 of 61 reactive block-0 attempts failed, 27 on slippage; "they landed, but behind other buyers" — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Vendor: RPC Fast says a failed *bundle* still pays the tip, and that `simulateBundle` is free and catches most state-mismatch failures before payment — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump). This conflicts with Jito's own guidance that an in-transaction tip is not paid on failure; the RPC Fast statement is correct only for a tip placed in a separate transaction of a bundle that lands while the buy fails, which Jito's atomicity rule prevents.

### Inferences
- Worked example at the internal p50 winner recipe (62k CU limit, 16M µL/CU, 0.003 SOL tip, one signature): a win costs ≈ 0.000005 + 0.001 + 0.003 ≈ 0.004 SOL; a landed slippage failure costs ≈ 0.001005 SOL (base + priority); a never-landed attempt costs 0. At the p90 recipe (160M µL/CU, 0.039 tip) a landed failure costs ≈ 0.0099 SOL. Shifting bid from priority fee to tip therefore cuts the cost of a lost race by the tip share, with no change to the win cost.
- Because the priority fee is charged on the requested limit, a 75k limit versus a 59k consumed (internal p50) overpays the priority leg by ~27% on every landed transaction, won or lost, and also lowers Agave/Firedancer priority (cost in the denominator) and Jito tip/CU ranking.
- Durable-nonce fan-out (same nonce on all sender variants) guarantees at most one landed copy, so at most one base fee, one priority fee and one tip are paid per attempt; without it, each duplicate that lands and fails on a guard or on slippage pays base + priority.
- Skipping `simulateTransaction` removes an RPC round trip but makes a doomed transaction land and pay; on contested launches the buy is often doomed anyway (22–38% slippage loss) so the saving is latency, and the price is one priority fee per lost race.

### Gaps
- Whether transactions that fail during account loading (as opposed to execution) are now included in blocks and charged (SIMD-0083/0191 "fee-only transactions") could not be confirmed from a primary source in this session; the Solana changelog describes SIMD-0191 as relaxing loading constraints so previously failing transactions can be included — [Solana Compass changelog](https://solanacompass.com/learn/Changelog/solana-changelog-nov-12-web3js-v2-skip-preflights-in-the-cli-and-simd-0191). Treat "insufficient SOL for the buy" failures as possibly charged.
- No source quantifies how much latency `simulateTransaction` adds for a colocated bot (vendors say preflight is a trade of validation for latency without numbers).
- Helius Sender and bloXroute do not state their behaviour when the transaction lands but reverts; the mechanism (in-tx transfer) implies the tip reverts, but this is inference.

## KQ4. Jito bundle vs plain priority-fee transaction: which lands earlier after the creator's bundle, by leader type?

### Takeaway
On Jito-Agave leaders (≈84% of stake runs Agave/Jito-Agave; 98.7% of stake is Jito-enabled; 37% of stake is on BAM) bundles are ranked against other bundles by tip per requested CU in 50 ms auctions while plain transactions are ranked by Agave's fee/cost formula in which tips are invisible; there is no documented rule placing bundles ahead of plain transactions, and measured ordering is dominated by arrival time on all client types, with Firedancer (≈15% of stake, no Jito tips, bundles FIFO) the most fee-priority-aligned.

### Cited Findings
Jito-Agave / Block Engine
- "Bundles compete against other bundles on tips to the validator." Parallel auctions run at 50 ms ticks; bundles whose locks intersect on an account ((w,w), (r,w), (w,r)) run in the same auction; within an auction ordering is by "requested tip and CU-requested efficiency"; Jito "submits the highest paying combination of bundles to the validator up to some CU limit"; minimum tip 1,000 lamports to one of 8 tip accounts; "Tips do not prioritize transactions for validators that are not Jito-Solana leaders"; for `sendTransaction` Jito recommends a 70/30 split between priority fee and tip; for `sendBundle` "only the Jito tip matters" — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/).
- BundleStage "acts as a parallel counterpart to the existing Solana transaction flow (like BankingStage)"; compute limits are "partially reduced during the first 80% of PoH ticks" to leave room for bundles, "if no bundles arrive, regular transactions can still use it"; the write-up gives no fixed block position for bundles and describes an older 200 ms tick — [thogiti, "How Jito-Solana Works" (Jan 2025)](https://thogiti.github.io/2025/01/01/How-Jito-Solana-Works.html).
- A secondary explainer claims the leader places the highest-tipped bundles at the top of the block — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/jito-bundles-explained); this is not stated in Jito's docs.
- Jito shut down its hosted Relayer fleet on 29 April 2026; validators now connect directly to the Block Engine — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-validator-client-comparison-jito-firedancer-harmonic-rakurai). Jito-Solana "runs on more than 95% of active stake" by mid-2026 — [Chainstack (updated 6 Oct 2026)](https://chainstack.com/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana/). Internal stakewiz pull: Jito-enabled stake 98.7%, client mix Agave/Jito-Agave 4.x ≈84%, Firedancer v26.x ≈15%, Frankendancer 0.3% — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §0.
- `jitodontfront`: any bundle containing a transaction with an account whose key starts with `jitodontfront` is rejected by the block engine unless that transaction is at index 0 of the bundle; it applies only to the block engine and is "not guaranteed" to stop sandwiching — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/).

Agave (plain transactions)
- Priority `P = R/(1+C)`, R = validator reward (priority fee + unburned half of base fee), C = cost-model cost; "attempts to sell blockspace to the highest bidder" — [agave source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/transaction_priority.rs). Anza's design proposal describes the same fee-per-requested-CU ranking and the rule that lower-priority transactions must not grab locks a blocked higher-priority transaction needs — [Anza proposal](https://docs.anza.xyz/proposals/fee_transaction_priority). The central scheduler has been default since Agave v2.0 — [Solana forum overview](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803).
- "Jito tips are invisible to this formula unless the transaction arrives through the block engine"; the scheduler drains a priority queue continuously, "so a high-fee transaction that arrives late is still placed late" — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.

Firedancer / Frankendancer
- `pack` orders by "reward / compute resources" with penalties for hot writable accounts and large allocations; bundles live in their own treap, are "generally ordered FIFO", are not mixed with votes and are scheduled only when no vote is scheduled — [Solana forum overview](https://forum.solana.com/t/a-practical-overview-of-scheduler-implementations-in-solana-validators/4803); a one-transaction bundle is "a transaction with extremely high priority" and durable-nonce conflicts are resolved by priority — internal citing [`fd_pack.h`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/firedancer-io/firedancer/main/src/disco/pack/fd_pack.h), `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.
- Frankendancer historically lacked Jito's bundle auction (operators "leaving MEV revenue on the table") — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-validator-client-comparison-jito-firedancer-harmonic-rakurai). FireBAM (BAM for Frankendancer, ≈12% of stake at the time) went live on testnet and mainnet for early onboarding on 13 May 2026, full release targeted July 2026 after audit; pure-Firedancer support "once Jump Crypto releases it" — [bam.dev, Introducing FireBAM](https://bam.dev/blog/introducing-firebam-bam-expands-to-firedancer/). Full Firedancer MEV bundle support is listed as "Pluggable (in progress)" — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-validator-client-comparison-jito-firedancer-harmonic-rakurai).
- Client stake shares, top 500 validators, 13 Oct 2026: Firedancer ≈6.8%, HarmonicFiredancer ≈7.1%, FireBAM ≈1.6%, Frankendancer ≈0.3% (≈15.8% combined) — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc).
- Alpenglow activates on mainnet 21 Oct 2026 at about 14:50 UTC; supported clients are Agave, Jito-Solana and Harmonic Salsa (built on Agave 4.3.0); "Firedancer and Frankendancer are not supported during the migration"; all BAM nodes enter Maintenance Mode in the days before activation (Multi-Packet Processing and Dynamic Block Incentives paused; DBI unavailable); operators told not to run FireBAM/FrankenBAM during the transition — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc); [Solana Compass on Jito requirements](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-details-bams-alpenglow-plan-maintenance-mode-for-all-mainnet-nodes-no-firebam-during-the-transition). Slot time was cut from 400 ms to 200 ms in four stages in October 2026, reaching 200 ms at epoch 1053 (9 Oct 2026) — same Solana Compass article.

BAM
- BAM nodes (SEV-SNP TEEs) run "frequent periodic auctions", sign the full sequence, and validators "must execute the pre-sequenced list in FIFO order, with no insertions or reordering"; today "apps can influence ordering only through priority fees or bundles with tips"; Jito charges 6% on tips — [Helius, BAM](https://www.helius.dev/blog/block-assembly-marketplace-bam).
- Under Direct Bundle Ingest (13 Oct 2026), bundles that pass signature checks and simulation "enter BAM's auction, ranked by total fees (priority fees plus Jito tips) relative to the compute requested"; DBI skips the roughly 50 ms Block Engine hop (about a quarter of a 200 ms slot); Block Engine traffic (bundles and `sendTransaction`) is forwarded to BAM through DBI; DBI is whitelist-only; BAM validators hold ≈164M SOL ≈37% of stake at epoch 1050 — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop). Internal BAM explorer pull: 386 validators, 37.6% of stake; BAM nodes by stake ams 10.2%, fra 5.3%, sqq 4.5%, tyo 4.2%, sin 3.7%, iad 3.5% — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §0.
- A 13 Oct 2026 BAM upgrade trimmed bundle submission delays by about 50 ms and moved auction intervals from 25 ms to 20 ms to match 200 ms slots — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/jito-bam-upgrade-cuts-bundle-delays/) (secondary; the 20 ms figure was not found in a Jito primary source).

Harmonic
- Default strategy `fba`: a 50 ms frequent batch auction; within each batch transactions are "sorted by descending priority fees and tips"; searcher bundles enter the same buffer and are ranked the same way; shreds are broadcast continuously throughout the leader slot — [Harmonic docs](https://docs.harmonic.gg/concepts/scheduling-strategies.md).

Senders' view of ordering
- Nozomi: on block-builder paths (Jito or Harmonic) "the tip is the primary lever" and drives ordering; with equal tips the first arrival wins; the priority fee "matters mainly on non-block-builder paths" — [Nozomi docs](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq).
- Helius Sender Max "enters you into a priority tip buffer for top of block" and "tip more to land first"; routes to "Helius, Jito, Harmonic, Rakurai, etc." — [Helius Sender docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender).
- Measurements: no leader class blocked a block-0 reaction (reaction rate BAM 19.1%, non-BAM Agave 21.0%, Firedancer 22.5%); leader geography mattered more (Europe 23.8%, North America 13.8%, APAC 9.0%) — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §6. Fee/CU predicted order only weakly: 54–58% on Agave/BAM, 56.6% per-(slot,mint) on Firedancer (87% raw, dominated by a few hot mints) — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §1.

### Inferences
- On a Jito-Agave leader, a single-transaction Jito bundle and a plain transaction do not compete in the same queue: the bundle is ranked by tip/CU among bundles in a 50 ms auction (plus the Block Engine hop), the plain transaction by R/(1+C) among the TPU stream. Which arrives at the bank first is decided mostly by path latency, not by the bid; the bundle path adds up to one 50 ms tick plus the ~50 ms Block Engine hop (unless DBI), which on a 200 ms slot is often the whole reaction budget.
- On BAM leaders the two paths converge: BAM ranks everything by (priority fee + tip)/requested CU in ~20–25 ms auctions, so a priority fee and a tip are substitutes for position, and the tip has the advantage of reverting on failure.
- On Firedancer leaders (≈15% of stake, more after pure-Firedancer BAM) Jito tips buy nothing; only fee per cost unit and arrival matter, and the Alpenglow migration temporarily removes Firedancer from the leader set anyway.
- "Pay-on-success" tips are positionally neutral on tip-ordered leaders (the ranking uses the requested tip, which is the same whether or not it ultimately reverts) and invisible on priority-ordered Agave leaders unless the transaction enters via the block engine or BAM.

### Gaps
- No primary Jito document states how Jito-Agave interleaves BundleStage output with BankingStage output inside the block; the "reserved compute for bundles in the first 80% of ticks" description is from a third-party deep dive dated Jan 2025 and may be outdated after BAM.
- No published measurement compares landing index of single-tx bundles versus plain priority-fee transactions sent at the same instant to the same Jito-Agave leader.
- BAM's auction interval (20 vs 25 ms) and its exact ranking formula are not published by Jito; `docs.bam.dev` did not resolve during this session.

## KQ5. What do known block-0 sniper tools do about fees, and what do they claim?

### Takeaway
Open-source and commercial snipers in 2026 converge on the same mechanics—fan out one durable-nonce transaction to many staked senders and builders, each with a 0.001 SOL minimum tip, and a dynamic rather than hardcoded tip—while fee defaults are either blank or set by third-party guides (0.001–0.02 SOL priority, 0.002–0.05 SOL bribe) and landing-rate claims (89–94% "slot 0") come only from vendors with no independent measurement.

### Cited Findings
Open-source bots
- D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot (Rust, "0 slot timings"): sends every buy simultaneously to all enabled providers—jito, helius-sender, bloxroute, nozomi (Temporal), blockrazor, falcon, flashblock, nextblock, node1, astralane, 0slot—using one durable nonce per concurrent buy so "at most one lands"; config knobs `SNIPER_TIP_MIN_SOL`, `SNIPER_TIP_MAX_SOL`, `SNIPER_DYNAMIC_TIP=1`, per-provider CU price and `SNIPER_CU_LIMIT` with blank defaults; detects creates from Jito ShredStream shred 0; claims detect 5–7 µs, signing ≈10 µs per provider thread, "execution under 1 ms"; publishes no landing-rate or cost statistics — [GitHub](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot).
- Older TypeScript bots (1fge/pump-fun-sniper-bot, 548 days stale) hardcode a priority fee; Chainstack's tutorial bot offers fixed or dynamic priority fee from recent prioritization fees — [1fge](https://github.com/1fge/pump-fun-sniper-bot); [Chainstack](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-creating-a-pumpfun-bot). iSyqozz/pump.fun-sniper: `.env` priority fee where "0 means the default high priority fees", Jito bundles optional — [GitHub](https://github.com/beersweetpicklepanda/pump.fun-sniper) (search result).

Sender tip floors (what every bot must pay per landed transaction)
- Jito bundles: 1,000 lamports minimum — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/).
- Helius Sender Max: 0.001 SOL minimum (enters the "priority tip buffer"); `swqos_only=true`: 0.000005 SOL, single SWQoS path, no buffer; tips in between are "best-effort through fewer pathways" — [Helius Sender Max](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender-max); [Helius Sender](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender).
- Nozomi: 0.001 SOL default minimum, below which transactions are silently dropped; lower per-account minimums for high-volume flows via dashboard — [Nozomi](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq).
- bloXroute Trader API: 0.001 SOL (1,000,000 lamports) minimum per transaction; `submit-snipe` takes two transactions (one to Jito, one over staked connections) with minimum Jito tip 0.001 SOL plus minimum bloXroute tip 0.0001 SOL — [bloXroute tips](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/trader-api/introduction/tip-and-tipping-addresses); [submit-snipe](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/trader-api/api-endpoints/core-endpoints/submit-snipe).
- Astralane: 10,000-lamport minimum per route per a third-party wrapper's docs — [ThorNode](https://docs.thornode.io/api-reference/bifrost/astralane); internal note from Astralane's own docs: 0.001 SOL free tier, 0.0001 VIP1–2, 0.00001 VIP3 (after 8 SOL of tips in 7 days) — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §3.
- 0slot: 0.001 SOL (0.0001 on the 50-TPS plan), homepage only, unverified — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §3.
- Observed sender mix of slot-0 winners (by tip account, internal Oct 2026): none/unknown 437, Helius 376, Astralane 139, Jito 126, Nozomi 123, Flashblock 55, Helius+Jito 41 — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4; 87-create study: Helius 15, Nozomi 12, Astralane 9, Jito 7, NextBlock 1, 46 with no known-provider tip (direct, staked or unknown senders such as `rapidX…`, `LandX…`) — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- Astralane also sells "Postpacks": notice from Harmonic leaders before execution, earlier than shreds — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §3.

Trading terminals (all figures from third-party guides; no official docs found)
- BullX: default priority fee ≈0.01 SOL, 0.015–0.02 during congestion; "bribe" (Jito tip) used in MEV-only mode with a 0.002 SOL minimum enforced, 0.01–0.015 suggested for competitive entries; with MEV mode on the bribe matters most — [coincodecap](https://coincodecap.com/best-settings-for-bullx-bot); [publish0x](https://www.publish0x.com/solana-trading-bots/bullx-fees-explained-xywpdkg). One 2026 guide says BullX trading was disabled on 1 June 2026 — [madeonsol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-bullx-alternatives) (unverified).
- Axiom: guides suggest 0.001 SOL priority for standard trades and a 0.01 SOL bribe "to jump crowded queues", MEV protection on (one guide says ≈0.04 SOL per transaction), 10–15% slippage for fresh pump.fun tokens; platform fee ≈0.75–0.95% — [GEMQUEENx](https://medium.com/@GEMQUEENx/axiom-trade-strategy-best-settings-filters-and-fees-for-maximum-profits-eab7504cb07d); [Lyncho setup guide](https://lynchocallouts.lovable.app/phantom-axiom-guide).
- Photon: 1% fee plus priority and bribe fees on top; no documented defaults found — [madeonsol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-sniping-tools).
- Trojan: no fee-setting documentation found; a Reddit guide gives 0.01 priority / 0.01 bribe for low capital and 0.15 / 0.15 for $100k+ volume tokens with MEV protection off — [r/SolanaSniperBots mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/SolanaSniperBots/hot).
- GMGN: priority fee settings "from 0.002 SOL standard to 0.006+ SOL for automated orders" — [Solana Compass project page](https://solanacompass.com/projects/GMGN).
- Internal note: a "0.05 SOL TradeWiz-style flat tip sits between the p75 (0.0165) and the p90 (0.068) of first-winner bids, so it overpays on most launches" — `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4 (TradeWiz default not confirmed from a vendor source).

Vendor landing-rate claims
- RPC Fast: co-located Frankfurt nodes landed in "slot 0" about 89% of the time over 240+ *migration* events (not creation slots); generic cloud <20%; target hot path <5 ms event-to-bundle; recommends multi-relay Jito submission (NY, Frankfurt, Tokyo, Amsterdam plus Astralane and QuickNode Lil-JIT), `jitodontfront` accounts and SWQoS staked priority — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump).
- Dysnix/RPC Fast "illustrative aggregates" from ~50,000 paired attempts, Q1 2026: first-10-confirmation rate 22% (public RPC) / 58% (shared) / 92% (RPC Fast); bundle landing rate <30% / 70% / 94%; revert rate during congestion 32% / 14% / 6%; submission p99 180–400 ms / 80–150 ms / 45 ms — [Dysnix](https://dysnix.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot).
- Dysnix: "more than 70% of sniper bots target sub-50 ms RPC latency, but only about 10% deliver consistent profits"; RPC Fast: "roughly 90% of DIY sniper bots fail" — cited in [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump).

### Inferences
- The 0.001 SOL sender minimum is itself above the internal p25 (0.0002 SOL) of all slot-0 winning bids and about 30% of the first-winner median, so simply fanning out to five paid senders without durable-nonce exclusivity would cost 0.005 SOL in tips per landed attempt if several copies landed; durable nonces make the fan-out cost one tip.
- The commercial terminals' "bribe" is a Jito/sender tip, so their users are bidding mostly in the tip-ordered path; their suggested 0.01 SOL bribe sits near the internal p75 first-winner bid, which matches the observation that terminal users are among the later same-slot winners.
- No vendor claim is for the pump.fun *creation* slot of a bundled launch; RPC Fast's 89% is for PumpSwap migrations, where the trigger (curve near 85 SOL) is visible seconds ahead.

### Gaps
- Official Photon, Trojan, Axiom, BullX and TradeWiz documentation of sniper fee defaults was not found; all numbers are from affiliate guides and may be stale.
- 0slot, NextBlock, Flashblock, BlockRazor tip minimums were not found in their own docs.

## KQ6. What landing rate does a well-built non-validator bot achieve in a contested creation slot?

### Takeaway
The only on-chain measurement found (internal, Oct 2026) puts per-wallet success at 46% of slot-0 reactive attempts overall, falling to about 62% loss on bundled repeat-creator launches once 38% slippage failures are counted, with reaction physically impossible for the ~7–17% of creates placed late in the block; vendor claims of 89–94% refer to migrations or to "first 10 confirmations", not to the creation slot.

### Cited Findings
- Per attempting wallet in slot 0: 46% won, 22% slippage-failed, 29% bot-guard aborts, 3% other; 38% slippage on bundled repeat-creator launches; slot +1: 12.6% slippage, 54.8% guard aborts; counting guard aborts as losses gives an upper bound of 51% of slot-0 attempts lost — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §3.
- 6.8% of creates sit in the last 5% of their block, where only 1.2% got a reactive buy; the last 15% holds 16.7% of creates with a 6.8% reaction rate against 23% elsewhere; leader geography: Europe 23.8%, North America 13.8%, APAC 9.0% reaction rate — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §6. The leader keeps the slot for up to 3 more slots, so slot +1 is the fallback (70 slot+1 buys seen in the 87-create study) — `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §2.
- The fastest repeat snipers land 5–9 transactions after the create, "the practical floor for a shred-reactive bot"; the first winner's median gap is 149 transactions (≈28 ms) — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §4.
- Stake geography: Frankfurt ≈32–34%, Amsterdam ≈19–21%, London 8.4%, Tokyo 5.6%, Šiauliai 4.6%, US-East ≈8%; about 70% of leader slots in a 1,600-slot sample were in Frankfurt/Amsterdam/London/Baltics — internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §0.
- Jito-wide bundle land rate 0.57% (1 in 175), simulation success 16.1% — spam-dominated and not comparable to a sniper's per-attempt rate — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop).
- Vendor: 89% slot-0 (migrations, RPC Fast internal benchmark), 92% first-10-confirmation and 94% bundle landing (Dysnix/RPC Fast "illustrative") — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump); [Dysnix](https://dysnix.com/blog/top-solana-sniper-bot).
- Pine Analytics (Apr 2025): deployer-linked same-block snipers were profitable on 87% of snipes (these are insiders with the create in hand, not reactive bots) — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines).
- Helius: staked connections "guarantee 100% transaction delivery" via SWQoS (Helius's own claim), and SWQoS gives connection slots by stake; a Solana Vibe Station note cited internally says SWQoS "doesn't put you in the front of the line" — [Helius docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/optimizing-transactions); internal, `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` §3.
- Economic context for the decision: being first is worth "roughly 6–15 points of break-even hit rate" because the 3rd/5th reactive buyer fills +10.8%/+15.8% above the post-bundle price; the naive 0.3 SOL end-of-slot-0 buy on random bundled launches returned −20% to −27% per trade net of fees — internal, `docs/research/gap-2.md` §5 and `data/reports/firstslot_tables.md`.

### Inferences
- A realistic planning number for a colocated (Frankfurt/Amsterdam) non-validator bot that bids at the internal p75 (≈0.014 SOL) on bundled launches is roughly a 45–60% landed-and-filled rate per attempt in slot 0, with most of the remainder landing-but-failing on slippage (paying base + priority fee) and a minority never landing (free). Raising the bid moves you within the batch but does not fix late block position or a distant leader, which cap the attainable rate around 80–90% even for a perfect bot.
- The expected fee cost per *filled* position is therefore roughly (win cost) + (loss probability / win probability) × (priority leg): at p75 recipe ≈ 0.014 + 1.0 × 0.0037 ≈ 0.018 SOL per fill; at p50 recipe ≈ 0.004 + 1.0 × 0.001 ≈ 0.005 SOL per fill.

### Gaps
- No independent, public measurement of a non-validator bot's creation-slot landing rate on contested pump.fun launches exists; vendor figures measure different events.
- The internal measurements are per observed wallet from one week and cannot see attempts that never landed, so the true attempt-level landing rate is lower than 46% by an unknown amount.

## Decision frame (synthesis for the writer)

- Position right after the creator's bundle is bought mainly with arrival time (colocation in FRA/AMS, direct-to-leader or BAM-node path, shred-0 detection) and only secondarily with money; measured fee-order correlation is 54–58% for all trades and 73% for contested snipes (internal) and Chorus One finds Agave leaders near random, Firedancer most priority-aligned — [Chorus One](https://chorus.one/reports-research/the-state-of-schedulers-on-solana-2).
- Size the bid from the creation-slot distribution, not the Jito tip floor: p50 ≈ 0.004 SOL, p75 ≈ 0.014–0.017 SOL, p90 ≈ 0.05–0.07 SOL for the first reactive winner (internal, Oct 2026). A flat 0.05 SOL overpays ~90% of the time.
- Put the at-risk part of the bid in the tip (reverts on failure per [Jito](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/) and [Nozomi](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq)) and keep the priority fee near the minimum the path needs; a lost race then costs ≈ base + priority only (internal median 0.00015 SOL for slippage losers).
- Set the CU limit to measured usage plus a small margin (≈59k consumed vs 75k requested at p50) and set the loaded-accounts-data-size limit tightly, because both the charged fee and Agave/Firedancer/BAM/Jito ranking use the *requested* cost — [Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/fee-structure.md); [agave source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/transaction_priority.rs).
- Fan out variants on one durable nonce so at most one lands and one tip is paid — [Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/durable-nonces.md); test it per path because Nozomi warns some builders deprioritise nonce transactions — [Nozomi](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq).
- Route by leader class: Jito-Agave (tip/CU in 50 ms bundle auction, fee/cost for plain txs), BAM (37% of stake; (fee+tip)/CU, FIFO execution, sit near the BAM node), Firedancer (≈15%; fee/cost only, tips wasted, bundles FIFO), Harmonic (50 ms FBA on fee+tip). Expect disruption around 21 Oct 2026 (Alpenglow): BAM in maintenance mode, Firedancer/Frankendancer out of the leader set during migration — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc).
