# Arrival Time Wins the Creation Slot

A Frankfurt bare-metal sniper can land a reactive buy in the pump.fun creation slot, behind the creator's atomic bundle, on most of the launches worth firing at, but only when three things line up: the leader is in Frankfurt, Amsterdam or elsewhere in Europe (roughly 70% of leader slots), the create executes in the first ~85% of the 200 ms slot, and the buy reaches the leader by a direct in-metro path (own QUIC to the leader's TPU or an in-metro staked sender over a warm connection), not through the Jito Block Engine. The operator's own 24-hour scan proves this is happening today: **22.1% of SOL-quoted creates and 62.2% of bundled, CEX-funded creates draw at least one independent same-slot winner**, the first winner lands a median **149 transactions (~28 ms) after the create**, and the earlier of two same-slot winners out-bid the later one in **73% of pairs** (internal). Where the research notes say same-slot reaction is "usually not feasible", they are right about four cases and wrong as a blanket statement: creates in the last ~15% of the block (16.7% of creates), leaders in North America or APAC, the legacy Block-Engine bundle path (a ~50 ms hop plus a 50 ms auction tick on a 200 ms slot), and Harmonic's 50 ms batch auctions. Position is bought first with arrival time and second with money: the winning first-reactive bid is a median **0.0034 SOL** (p75 0.0165, p90 0.068), the Jito network tip floor (p50 0.0000078 SOL) is irrelevant as a sizing signal, and the two free levers are a 62–65k CU request with a tight loaded-accounts-data-size limit (≈20% more scheduler priority per lamport) and putting the at-risk part of the bid in a tip that reverts on failure. Alpenglow on 21 October changes finality and the slot clock, not propagation or ordering; the practical regime changes that week are BAM nodes in maintenance mode (~37% of stake reverting to classic Block-Engine ordering) and Firedancer-family leaders (~15.8% of stake) leaving the schedule. For a budget of a few hundred to a few thousand USD per month the ranked plan is: measure the in-slot position and path-landing distribution for free this week, buy one budget shred feed ($199–500) and one premium feed on trial, build the direct-TPU lane, trim the transaction, then model the bid; skip validators, staking, Paladin and private stake leases entirely.

Provenance convention used throughout: **[M]** = measurement from primary code, official documentation or an independent on-chain pull; **[V]** = vendor or competitor claim with no independent replication; **[I]** = the operator's own internal on-chain data in `docs/research/gap-2.md` (3,581 creates, 24 h, 13–14 Oct 2026) and `docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md` (87 creates, 6.5 min). All slot arithmetic assumes the 200 ms slots live since epoch 1053 on 9 Oct 2026 (observed ~213–222 ms) ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solanas-200ms-slot-time-is-live-on-mainnet-at-epoch-1053-completing-simd-0525)).

## Same-slot reaction already happens on EU leaders; the "not feasible" verdict only holds for late creates, far leaders and slow paths

The pessimistic reading in the notes rests on one measurement applied out of context. Anza's February 2026 landing study found a mean **0.29 slots of landing latency from Amsterdam** over a staked connection, which at the then-400 ms slot is ~100–115 ms of wall clock, and the leader-ordering note infers from this that a Frankfurt bot reacting to shreds "will in most cases miss the creation slot entirely" at 200 ms ([Anza](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/transaction-landing-on-tpu)) [M]. But that 0.29-slot figure averages sends into leaders everywhere in the world, from a sender that was not trying to be first; the same study reports **0.25 ± 0.9 slots for same-region (AMS→EU) sends**, and the in-metro QUIC handshake to a co-located validator has been measured at **0.965 ms** ([Glassnode](https://latency.glassnode.com/solana/about)) [M]. The field's own benchmark pings put every major sender's Frankfurt ingress at **0.07–0.16 ms** from a TeraSwitch-class host ([vvizardev adapter](https://github.com/vvizardev/solana-relayer-adapter-rust)) [V]. Once the submit hop is ~1 ms rather than ~100 ms, the budget inside a 200 ms slot becomes: create executes at t, Agave coalesces entries for up to 50 ms before shredding, the shred reaches a Frankfurt relay subscriber ~2–10 ms later, decode and sign are sub-millisecond, the packet reaches the leader in ~1 ms, and the Agave scheduler picks it up within one sub-millisecond tick because receives are non-blocking while leader ([agave broadcast_utils.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/turbine/src/broadcast_stage/broadcast_utils.rs); [agave receive_and_buffer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/core/src/banking_stage/transaction_scheduler/receive_and_buffer.rs)) [M]. That leaves roughly **100–180 ms of a 200 ms slot** for a create that executed early, and nothing for one that executed late. The internal data matches that model precisely.

Three internal numbers settle it. First, geography: leaders in Europe drew a same-slot reaction on **23.8% of creates, North America 13.8%, APAC 9.0%**, and restricted to creates placed before the 85% mark Europe reaches **27.2%** (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §6) [I]. Second, in-block position: the **last 5% of the block holds 6.8% of creates and only 1.2% of them drew a reaction; the last 15% holds 16.7% of creates with a 6.8% reaction rate against 23% elsewhere** [I]. Third, leader class does not matter: non-BAM Agave 21.0%, BAM Agave 19.1%, Firedancer 22.5%, BAM Firedancer 20.3%, so BAM's 20 ms rounds do not block same-slot snipes [I]. The physically addressable fraction for a Frankfurt box is therefore roughly 70% of slots (EU leaders) times ~83–85% of in-slot positions, about **55–60% of all creates**. The 22.1% population rate is low not because reaction is infeasible but because professionals do not fire at most launches: wallets that won in three or more launches made **86% of slot-0 winning buys**, and the launches they target, bundled and CEX-fresh, show **62.2% same-slot reaction with a median 3.4 wallets attempting**, which is close to that addressable ceiling (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §2) [I]. The 46% per-wallet win rate is likewise not an inclusion statistic: of attempting wallets, 22% landed but failed on pump slippage (error 6042 dominating 6002 by 732 to 121 transactions) and 29% were on-chain bot-guard aborts; a winner sat at a lower index than a slippage loser in only 61% of pairs, so most losers set `min_tokens_out` against a price the bundle had already moved [I]. Same-slot inclusion is the common case for a well-placed bot; losing is mostly a pricing error.

The 5–9 transaction gap deserves a different explanation than the notes give it. At the observed block density (149 transactions ≈ 13% of the block implies ~1,150 transactions per block), 5–9 transactions is under 1% of the block, roughly 1–2 ms at uniform packing, and the index-to-time mapping is itself only approximate because Agave records entries from four worker threads and SIMD-0083 lets conflicting transactions share an entry ([Helius Agave 3.0](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v3-0)) [M]. No shred-fed reaction can close a 1–2 ms gap when entry coalescing alone takes several to 50 ms. The mechanics in jito-solana explain what those wallets did: banking-stage transactions that touch an account a bundle holds get `AccountInUse` and are retried immediately, so **the first post-bundle buy on the curve is the highest-priority conflicting buy already buffered at the leader when the bundle's lock cleared** ([jito-solana consumer.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/banking_stage/consumer.rs); [bundle_account_locker.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jito-foundation/jito-solana/master/core/src/bundle_stage/bundle_account_locker.rs)) [M]. A buy can only be buffered before the create executes if its sender had the mint before shreds existed: from a pre-execution signal (BAM preconfirmations run **5–10 ms ahead of shreds** and Helius preconfirmations fire when the leader executes or when a BAM node commits, both before shredding ([bam.dev](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/preconfirmations/); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/pre-confirmations/overview)) [V]), from the creator's own flow (1.1% of launches had a transaction touching the mint before the create, and the field admits that creators who separate create from dev-buy "can be front-run by other snipers" ([cicere/pumpfun-bundler](https://github.com/cicere/pumpfun-bundler)) [V]), or from a relay that forwards the create itself. The realistic floor for a shred-reactive bot is the internal **p10–p25 of the first-winner gap, 44–82 transactions or ~9–15 ms**, not 5–9. The 5–9 cases are a reason to buy a preconfirmation feed on BAM leaders, not a target for shred latency work.

What the field achieves beyond this is unknowable from public sources. No open-source or commercial bot publishes a measured landing rate, slot-offset histogram or detect-to-land time; the only hard numbers are in-process microsecond timings (D3AD-E reports 5–7 µs detect and ~10 µs per-provider signing) and self-reported "70–80% first buyer" claims with no sample ([D3AD-E](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot); [hodlwarden](https://github.com/hodlwarden/pumpfun-sniper)) [V]. Nozomi is the only sender advertising a **median 0-slot landing with p99 of 1 slot**, against "a performance-oriented client" ([Temporal](https://temporal.xyz/services/nozomi)) [V]; RPC Fast's **89% slot-0** figure is for PumpSwap migrations, where the trigger is visible seconds ahead, and synthetic migration has since removed that race ([RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/how-to-launches-snipe-pump)) [V]. Pine Analytics' **87% profitable same-block snipes** describe deployer-funded wallets with the create in hand, not reactive bots ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)) [M, 2025 data]. The honest field position is that same-slot reactive landing is possible for a shred-fed, co-located bot on staked or direct paths, that no non-insider has published a positive track record doing it, and that creator tooling ("Sniper Dump": dev-only buy, then a 100% sell when sniper volume arrives) is optimized against exactly this buyer ([cicere/pumpfun-bundler](https://github.com/cicere/pumpfun-bundler)) [V]. The internal rank-premium table is what makes rank 1 worth fighting for: the first reactive winner fills a median **+1.1%** over the post-bundle price, the third **+10.8%** and the fifth **+15.8%**, which maps to roughly **6–15 points of break-even hit rate** (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §5) [I].

## Detection: the leader's 50 ms coalesce window sets the floor, shreds beat gRPC by 5–11 ms at the median and far more at the tail

The leader does not shred a transaction the instant it executes. Agave's broadcast stage coalesces entries for up to `ENTRY_COALESCE_DURATION = 50 ms`, or until the batch reaches twice the typical erasure-batch byte count, or the slot ends; every FEC set is then padded to exactly 32 data plus 32 coding shreds and dispatched at once ([agave broadcast_utils.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/turbine/src/broadcast_stage/broadcast_utils.rs); [agave merkle.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/ledger/src/shred/merkle.rs)) [M]. Busy slots fill the byte target early, so a pump.fun create in a dense block leaves the leader a few milliseconds after execution; quiet slots pay the full 50 ms. Jito-Agave inherits this code; Firedancer's cadence is undocumented. A decoder does not need the whole FEC set: Agave's own read path, Jito's shredstream-proxy deshredder and the 0xfnzero `sol-shred-sdk` all emit entries as soon as every data shred from the previous `DATA_COMPLETE_SHRED` flag through the next one is present, ignoring coding shreds unless something was lost ([agave blockstore.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/ledger/src/blockstore.rs); [jito-labs/shredstream-proxy deshred.rs](https://github.com/jito-labs/shredstream-proxy/blob/master/proxy/src/deshred.rs); [sol-shred-sdk](https://github.com/0xfnzero/sol-shred-sdk)) [M]. Because the flag sits on the last data shred of the coalesced batch, the create and everything coalesced with it arrive as one unit; speculative parsing of a single data shred could in principle extract a transaction wholly contained in its payload, but no open-source decoder does this and the gain is at most the intra-batch arrival spread of a few milliseconds. D3AD-E's design of emitting "on shred 0 of a 79-shred segment, not shred 78" is the practical version of this ([D3AD-E](https://github.com/D3AD-E/Solana-sniper-bot)) [V].

Between the leader and a Frankfurt box, the relay matters less than the vendors say and more than the gRPC path admits. Every published comparison is a vendor- or competitor-run first-arrival race, and they agree on magnitudes: at TeraSwitch FRA2, Shreder measures DoubleZero Edge **p50 1.69 ms, p95 15 ms, p99 24 ms behind** its own feed, Helius Raw Shred Delivery **p50 2.31 ms**, and the defunct Jito ShredStream **p50 4.6 ms, p99 51 ms** ([Shreder benchmarks](https://shreder.xyz/benchmarks/)) [V]. The best feeds therefore sit within single-digit milliseconds of each other at the median with **25–55 ms p99 tails**, which is a quarter of a slot and the real reason to run two feeds and take first arrival (Triton blends sources this way itself) ([Triton](https://triton.one/products/triton-shred-streaming)) [V]. Against the operator's current Yellowstone gRPC `processed` subscription, shred-native feeds lead by **5–11 ms median and 17–50 ms at p95/p99** in Frankfurt (RPC Fast's Aperture race: first on 99.97% of 20,000 transactions, mean lead 10.7 ms, p99 50.7 ms; Helius quotes "about 8 ms" for its preprocessed stream) ([RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/benchmarks); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/laserstream)) [V]. The field's larger figures (Dysnix 32.8 ms mean lead over 2.08 million transactions; RPC Fast "120 ms average, 270 ms tail") compare shreds against gRPC on nodes that are not co-located ([Dysnix](https://dysnix.com/blog/how-to-build-low-latency-infrastructure-for-solana-ai-agents); [RPC Fast](https://docs.rpcfast.com/solana/jito-shredstream-grpc)) [V]. For the operator the honest expectation is: shreds move the median by one scheduling batch, which decides rank 1 versus rank 2–3 inside a contested slot, and they remove a p95 tail that today costs the slot outright on late-but-reachable creates. Running an own unstaked node does not help: unstaked nodes are placed at the bottom of the Turbine tree by design ([solana-labs PR #20480](https://github.com/solana-labs/solana/pull/20480)) [M], so a licensed multicast or relay feed in the same facility beats self-hosting as a detection source.

Pre-shred signals are the only way under the coalesce window, and they are partial. Helius Preconfirmations fire when a Helius-forwarding leader executes a transaction or a BAM validator commits to it, before shredding, over `preconfSubscribe` at 10 credits per message with a 300-credit minimum per active slot per subscription, now with BAM and signer filters on all paid plans ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/pre-confirmations/overview); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/helius-opens-solana-preconfirmations-to-all-paid-plans-and-adds-four-new-filters)) [V]. BAM's native preconfirmation stream (via Helius and Triton) is quoted at **p50 5–10 ms ahead of shred streams** and covers only BAM leaders, ~37% of stake ([bam.dev](https://bam.dev/docs/bam/preconfirmations/)) [V]. Shreder sells a "Preconfs" add-on claiming 5–50 ms ahead of leader shreds ([Shreder](https://shreder.xyz/products/)) [V], and Astralane sells "Postpacks", notice from Harmonic leaders before execution ([Astralane](https://astralane.gitbook.io/docs/low-latency/postpacks.md)) [V]. None publishes coverage or latency distributions, and the 300-credit-per-slot floor can be expensive on a program as busy as pump.fun; this is an experiment to run with tight signer filters on the tracked-creator list, not a default feed. A shred feed also has a structural blind spot the gRPC path does not: creates routed through another program are not visible to a program-id filter on raw transaction bytes ([chainstacklabs](https://github.com/chainstacklabs/pumpfun-bonkfun-bot)) [V], so gRPC stays as the fallback and as the source of execution metadata that shreds lack.

The feed market for a non-validator in Frankfurt or Amsterdam is now entirely paid. Jito shut ShredStream down on 5 September 2026 and pointed users to DoubleZero Edge ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/doublezero-removes-unauthorized-shred-retransmitters-claims-70-plus-lead-over-all-competitors)) [M]. Public prices: **DoubleZero Edge $1,500/month per machine in Frankfurt or Amsterdam** (multicast over private fiber, GRE/BGP/PIM onboarding, internal-use-only terms, 1–3 business days), **Triton $1,500/month per IP** in FRA/AMS, **Helius Raw Shreds $1,000 ($800 on Pro)**, **OrbitFlare $1,000** premium regions, **BlockRazor $500/stream/month**, **Everstake Connect $199 (Pro $499)**, **GetBlock $300**; Shreder is quote-only ([DoubleZero docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/solana/edge/subscribe/); [Triton](https://triton.one/products/triton-shred-streaming); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/laserstream); [OrbitFlare](https://docs.orbitflare.com/shredstream); [BlockRazor](https://docs.blockrazor.io/streams/block-stream/solana/shred-stream); [Everstake](https://docs.blockspace.everstake.com/shredstream); [GetBlock](https://getblock.io/v2/products/shred-streams)) [V]. All are unicast UDP to a public IPv4:port you register (no NAT) except DoubleZero's multicast. The honest caveat is that no independent "slot start to first shred in Frankfurt" distribution exists for any of them; every number above is a two-feed race published by one of the two.

## Submission: own QUIC to the leader first, in-metro senders second, the Block Engine last, and each leader class orders differently

An unstaked Frankfurt box can connect straight to the leader's TPU and will usually be accepted. Agave master gives unstaked peers a 3,000-connection pool (2,000 in 3.1), **8 connections per IP per minute**, **128 concurrent streams** per connection, a quota of **40 streams per 100 ms that collapses to 10 when the leader is loaded**, a 30 s idle timeout, and oldest-first pruning when the pool fills ([agave quic.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/streamer/src/quic.rs); [swqos.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/streamer/src/nonblocking/swqos.rs); [stream_throttle.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/streamer/src/nonblocking/stream_throttle.rs)) [M]. A sniper sends a handful of packets per second, so the binding constraint is eviction and handshake churn at the moment of a hot launch, not throughput: keep at most 8 warm connections per IP, open them 4–8 slots (~0.8–1.6 s) ahead as Anza's `tpu-client-next` does, send only to the current leader in slots 1–3 of its window and to current plus next in slot 4 ([Anza](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/transaction-landing-on-tpu)) [M]. Triton's Jet sender is open source as the Yellowstone Jet TPU crate and already implements leader tracking and pre-connection ([Triton](https://blog.triton.one/swqos-for-everyone-why-we-retired-cascade-marketplace/)) [V]. Stake does not buy priority inside the slot: SWQoS reserves 80% of TPU capacity for staked peers by stake share, Triton reports staked and unstaked "nearly the same" at p50, and under Agave 4.0 SWQoS becomes a congestion backstop that engages near 90% TPU load ([Solana SWQoS guide](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/stake-weighted-qos); [Triton](https://blog.triton.one/swqos-for-everyone-why-we-retired-cascade-marketplace/)) [V]. A staked identity or a Frankfurt-local SWQoS endpoint is insurance against the congestion that a hot launch produces, which is worth a few hundred dollars but not a landing-rate edge in itself; the public options are ERPC's Frankfurt shared endpoint (€42–598/month plans) and Everstake SWQoS at $500–1,300/month, none with a published uplift ([ERPC](https://erpc.global/en/staked-connection/); [Everstake](https://everstake.com/products/swqos)) [V]. On a BAM leader the advertised TPU is the BAM node, so "direct to leader" lands on the BAM node and the hop that matters is to the BAM node's region (internal BAM explorer pull: ams 10.2% of stake, fra 5.3%, sqq 4.5%; internal: [speed-tx-landing.md](../docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md) §0) [I].

The in-metro senders are the second lane and each has a Frankfurt and an Amsterdam ingress; the differences are fee model and policy. Nozomi recommends its direct `fra2`/`ams1` hosts over plain HTTP with Batch Send ("QUIC is not faster than a warm HTTP connection"), new keys get 5 req/s per region, sends to several regions do not share a limit, below-minimum tips are silently dropped, server-side retries are included, client retries lower priority, and **builders may deprioritize durable-nonce transactions** ([Nozomi docs](https://use.temporal.xyz/llms-full.txt); [Nozomi troubleshooting](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/troubleshooting)) [V]. Astralane's `fr`/`ams` gateways accept QUIC on the IPv4, its routers are pure FIFO at every tier, and its rebates fall for flow "shared with other RPCs or landing services" or using an advanced nonce ([Astralane endpoints](https://astralane.gitbook.io/docs/low-latency/endpoints-and-configs.md); [Astralane rebates](https://astralane.gitbook.io/docs/low-latency/submit-transactions/tip-refunds.md)) [V]. BlockRazor Frankfurt runs `fast` mode at 3 TPS default with a 0.0001 SOL minimum tip and a `revertProtection` flag that it warns makes inclusion slower ([BlockRazor](https://docs.blockrazor.io/transaction-submission/fast/solana/send-transaction)) [V]. Helius Sender's `fra-`/`ams-sender` hosts require both a tip and a compute-unit price, 50 req/s with a key, and claim "≤1.5 slots" typical land time, which is a next-slot claim, not a same-slot one ([Helius Sender](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender.md); [Helius FAQ](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/sender.md)) [V]. NextBlock adds raw QUIC on `frankfurt.nextblock.io:11100` at $249–1,749/month ([NextBlock](https://docs.nextblock.io/llms-full.txt)) [V]. The internal sender mix of slot-0 winners is the only measurement of who actually lands: **none/unknown 437, Helius 376, Astralane 139, Jito 126, Nozomi 123, Flashblock 55**, where the 437 are direct, staked or private senders with no public tip account (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §4) [I]. The largest single bucket is the lane the operator does not yet have. Jito's regional engines, Harmonic, Astralane and BlockRazor all publish 64.130.x.x (TeraSwitch) addresses and Glassnode attributes ~59% of leader slots to TeraSwitch hosting, so a box in TeraSwitch Frankfurt or Cherry Servers via its PNI (sub-0.25 ms to Jito's Frankfurt servers, per Cherry) is where the sub-millisecond hops come from ([Glassnode](https://latency.glassnode.com/solana/about); [Cherry Servers](https://www.cherryservers.com/blog/pni-with-teraswitch)) [V].

The Jito Block Engine is the wrong primary path for a reactive same-slot buy and the right path for a revert-protected slow variant. Jito's own docs put the Block-Engine hop at "roughly 50 ms" and run auctions on 50 ms ticks; bundles that touch the same writable account compete in one auction ranked by tip per requested CU; `sendTransaction` is always forwarded as a bundle with skip_preflight and `bundleOnly=true` buys revert protection; the default rate limit is 1 request/s per IP per region ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop)) [M]. Up to 100 ms of a 200 ms slot before the bundle reaches the leader means the bundle lane realistically targets the next slot of the same leader. Direct Bundle Ingest removes the Block-Engine hop by letting searchers submit bundles to a BAM node over QUIC, ranked by (priority fee + tip) per requested CU, but it is **whitelist-only via a form, available only on BAM leaders, and switched off during the Alpenglow maintenance window**; Jito's own before/after shows end-to-end bundle land rate moving from 0.45% to 0.57% and average tip per landed bundle rising 72% ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-announces-bam-direct-bundle-ingest-routing-solana-bundles-past-the-block-engines-50ms-hop)) [V]. Apply for DBI, but do not plan around it. No service on Solana offers a target-relative backrun ("place me after signature X"): Jito's mempool backrun example is discontinued, `jitodontfront` only protects index 0 of a bundle, and bloXroute's `submit-snipe` is two of your own transactions on a shared nonce ([jito-labs/searcher-examples](https://github.com/jito-labs/searcher-examples); [bloXroute](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/trader-api/api-endpoints/core-endpoints/submit-snipe)) [M]. Rebuilding a bundle `[creator_create_tx, my_buy, tip]` from the shredded create is permitted by the rules but fails whenever the create is already inside the creator's own bundle, which is 35.2% of creates (internal) [I]; the buy must be a standalone transaction that executes after the bundle.

How a leader orders the buy once it arrives depends on the client, and the operator should route and bid per leader class. The table summarizes the mechanics; the Chorus One footrule study is the independent check: Agave leaders are "arrival-dominated" (distance ~0.66, near random), BAM ~0.6 (priority reflected only within small batches), Firedancer ~0.40–0.45 and clearly priority-aligned ([Chorus One](https://chorus.one/reports-research/the-state-of-schedulers-on-solana-2)) [M].

Two consequences follow. On non-BAM Jito-Agave leaders, which are the plurality, the only in-slot lever for a TPU-sent buy is the compute-unit price and the requested cost, and "right behind the bundle" is a matter of being the highest-priority conflicting buy already in the queue when `AccountInUse` clears; sending both a TPU priority transaction and a one-transaction `bundleOnly` bundle covers both queues, and the bundle carries the revert-protected tip-heavy bid. On BAM leaders, tip and priority fee are substitutes and the tip has the advantage of reverting on failure. The internal pairwise data is consistent with this split: among all pump trades the earlier transaction had the higher fee per CU only 54% of the time on non-BAM Agave and 58% on BAM, but **73% among contested slot-0 snipes**, which is what you expect when snipers cluster in the same scheduling batch and the fee reorders within it (internal: [speed-tx-landing.md](../docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md) §1; [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §4) [I].

## Bid sizing: 0.003–0.017 SOL wins most launches, the tip is the at-risk leg, and requested compute is the free lever

The pump.fun creation slot is a separate fee market from Solana at large, and the public signals describe the wrong market. The Jito tip floor on 18 October read **p50 7,760 lamports, p95 0.00015 SOL, p99 0.00102 SOL** across all landed bundles ([Jito tip_floor](https://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_floor)) [M], while the internal first-reactive-winner bid (priority fee plus tips) is **p25 0.001, p50 0.0034, p75 0.0165, p90 0.068 SOL**, roughly 400× the Jito median; across all slot-0 winners the p50 is 0.0018 SOL and in contested launches the first winner's median bid is 0.006 SOL against 0.0011 for later winners (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §4) [I]. Nozomi's live floor (p50 0.001, p95 0.01, p99 0.02 SOL) and NextBlock's documented sample (p50 0.005, p99 0.48 SOL) show where competitive sniping flow actually bids ([Nozomi tip_floor](https://api.nozomi.temporal.xyz/tip_floor); [NextBlock](https://docs.nextblock.io/llms-full.txt)) [V]. Retail terminals' suggested 0.01 SOL "bribe" sits at the internal p75, which matches their users being among the later same-slot winners ([coincodecap on BullX](https://coincodecap.com/best-settings-for-bullx-bot)) [V]. A flat 0.05 SOL tip overpays on roughly 90% of launches. Sizing must come from the operator's own per-launch history, bucketed by bundle size and leader class: the internal recommendation of about 0.005–0.02 SOL for the target class and planning for the p90 (0.04–0.07 SOL) only on bundled CEX-fresh launches is the right shape.

Where the bid sits in the transaction changes what a lost race costs. The protocol charges the 5,000-lamport base fee plus the full priority fee on the **requested** CU limit whenever a transaction executes, even if it fails, and advances the durable nonce; a transaction that never lands costs nothing ([Solana fee structure](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees/fee-structure.md); [Solana durable nonces](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions/durable-nonces.md)) [M]. A tip that is a transfer instruction inside the same transaction reverts with it, so Jito's and Nozomi's guidance is that a failed transaction pays no tip ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/); [Nozomi](https://use.temporal.xyz/nozomi/tipping-and-faq)) [M]. The internal failure economics confirm the split: slippage losers paid a median **0.00015 SOL** of priority fee and nothing else [I]. At the p50 winner recipe (62k CU, 16M µL/CU ≈ 0.001 SOL priority, 0.003 SOL tip) a win costs ~0.004 SOL and a landed loss ~0.001 SOL; at the p90 recipe a landed loss costs ~0.01 SOL. Shift bid from priority fee to tip wherever the path honors tips (BAM, Harmonic via senders, Jito bundles, Nozomi, Helius Max), and keep the priority leg at the path's floor (Helius requires 5,000 lamports and recommends 10,000; Nozomi and BlockRazor recommend ≥1,000,000 µL/CU) ([Helius Sender Max](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender-max.md); [BlockRazor](https://docs.blockrazor.io/transaction-submission/transaction-sending/solana/priority-fee-and-tip)) [V]. The exception is the non-BAM Agave TPU lane, where the tip buys nothing and the priority fee is the whole bid. Fan-out on one durable nonce guarantees that at most one copy lands and one tip is paid; the sender minimums (0.001 SOL at Nozomi, Helius Max, Astralane free tier, 0slot default, bloXroute; 0.0001 at BlockRazor; 0.00001 at Astralane VIP3 after 8 SOL of tips in 7 days; 5,000 lamports for Helius `swqos_only`; 1,000 lamports for Jito bundles) are service prices, not market-clearing tips ([Astralane tiers](https://astralane.gitbook.io/docs/low-latency/send-txn-fee-tiers.md); [Helius Sender](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender.md)) [V].

The free priority comes from the cost model. Agave's priority divides reward by the cost model's total, which includes the requested CU limit and a charge of 8 CU per 32 KiB of the requested loaded-accounts-data-size limit; the default 64 MiB adds **16,384 CU of phantom cost**, and setting it to the observed 12–16 MiB saves ~13k cost units, about **20% more priority per lamport on a ~62k CU buy**; the internal scan found at least 56% of pump buys never set it ([agave cost_model.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/cost-model/src/cost_model.rs); internal: [speed-tx-landing.md](../docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md) §1) [M/I]. Reactive slot-0 winners consume a median **59k CU** for the whole transaction but request 75k, overpaying the priority leg by ~27% and lowering their rank in every fee-per-CU auction (Agave, Firedancer, Jito tip/CU, BAM fee/CU) (internal) [I]. `buy_v3` consumes ~51k CU against ~117k for legacy `buy`, needs the user's token ATA to exist (so add `createAssociatedTokenAccountIdempotent`), and the `user_volume_accumulator` should be created once ahead of time ([pump TRADE_V3.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/main/docs/instructions/TRADE_V3.md)) [M]. Version-1 transactions carry the compute config in the header and 41% of block-0 buys already use them; tip accounts must never sit in an address lookup table because Jito and others do not detect them there ([Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/)) [M]. The per-account write limit (24M CU on Agave master, ≥20M on the conservative reading at 200 ms slots) would need ~200 buys of 100k CU on one curve in one block before cutting anyone off, so it is not the binding constraint; block pacing and time are ([agave block_cost_limits.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anza-xyz/agave/master/cost-model/src/block_cost_limits.rs)) [M].

Slippage, not inclusion, is where most of the money is lost. Set `max_sol_cost` from the curve state after the expected number of rivals ahead, size the tolerance to the rank you are actually buying (the rank-3 premium is a median 10–16%), and cap fills at about **+10% over the post-bundle price**, because paying the rank-3 or rank-5 premium raises break-even to 57–66% against base rates measured elsewhere of 7–13% (internal: [gap-2.md](../docs/research/gap-2.md) §5, §8) [I]. The on-chain bot-guard programs that abort 29% of slot-0 attempts at a median 0.000005 SOL of priority fee are the cheap-spray version of this discipline: fire at many creates, abort inside your own program when the price has moved, pay almost nothing [I].

## Alpenglow changes finality and the clock, not propagation; the week of 21 October is a regime change for other reasons

What activates at epoch 1064 on Wednesday 21 October (~14:50–15:00 UTC) is Votor: off-chain BLS votes, a fast-finalization certificate at 80% of stake (~100 ms) or a two-round finalized certificate at 60% (~150 ms), `confirmed` collapsing into `finalized`, vote transactions and PoH tick entries leaving blocks, and multiple candidate banks per slot tagged by `bank_id` ([solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/alpenglow)) [M]. Turbine, the shred wire format, FEC sets and every shred feed stay exactly as they are; SIMD-0326 says "Initially we stay with Turbine" and Rotor "will get its own SIMD" with no date ([SIMD-0326](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/main/proposals/0326-alpenglow.md)) [M]. Slot time stays 200 ms and the leader window stays four slots (800 ms). For the sniper, 22 October's shred stream differs from 21 October's in four ways: no tick entries, so any "how far into the slot are we" heuristic based on `SHRED_TICK_REFERENCE_MASK` or tick counts breaks and must become a local slot clock corrected for latency to the leader, as Anza recommends; no vote transactions, so entry batches are smaller and the 50 ms coalesce timeout binds more often in quiet slots; block header and footer markers appear in the stream and entry readers skip them; and `(slot, bank_id)` becomes the key once Agave 4.4's fast leader handover (mainnet target 9 November) lets a leader restart its block mid-slot on `UpdateParent`, expected in under 1% of slots but enough that a fill seen in shreds for a discarded bank vanishes ([solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/alpenglow-activates-on-solana-testnet-as-frankendancer-era-ends-agave-v44-schedule-targets-november-9-mainnet-activation)) [M]. Yellowstone `v16.0.0-rc10+solana.4.3.0` has the bank-aware buffering ([solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow)) [M].

The activation itself is a trading pause. The gate flips at the epoch boundary, TowerBFT runs for 5,000 more slots (~18 minutes), blocks then carry only votes until a genesis certificate at 82% of stake forms, validators roll back and hand off to Votor at slot 459,653,000, and user transactions resume; Anza expects the handoff to take "hundreds of milliseconds" but the certificate can stall if stake is slow to vote ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc)) [M]. Gate resumption on `getAgGenesisCert` returning non-null on your own node, not on the clock ([solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow)) [M]. The slip risk is client share: ~15.8% of stake sat on Firedancer-family labels on 13 October and Firedancer does not support the migration, leaving ~84% against an 82% threshold ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc)) [M].

The regime changes that actually alter the landing race that week come from the clients, not the consensus. Jito puts every mainnet BAM node into Maintenance Mode "in the days leading up to activation": connected validators revert to ordinary Jito-Agave Block-Engine ordering, bundles keep working, DBI and the Maker plugin are unavailable, and nodes return "gradually" afterwards, so for several days roughly **37% of stake stops ordering by (fee + tip)/CU in 20 ms rounds and goes back to Agave's fee/cost scheduler where tips on plain transactions buy nothing** ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jito-details-bams-alpenglow-plan-maintenance-mode-for-all-mainnet-nodes-no-firebam-during-the-transition)) [M]. Firedancer, Frankendancer, FireBAM and FrankenBAM leave the leader set during the migration, removing the most fee-aligned ~15.8% of stake, and the client mix to model becomes Agave, Jito-Agave and Harmonic Salsa ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-alpenglow-mainnet-date-solanas-consensus-switch-is-scheduled-for-october-21-at-about-1450-utc)) [M]. After activation, exit logic gains two things: finality ~100–150 ms after production instead of ~12.8 s, so a sell gated on `confirmed` can wait for `finalized` with little added delay and near-zero rollback risk; and a Skip Certificate on a leader's first slot collapses that leader's whole 800 ms window, which is a hard "re-route to the next leader now" signal that today's soft fork-and-repair heuristics lack ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/alpenglow)) [M]. Rotor, when it ships, concentrates relaying in high-stake nodes and is multicast-friendly, which strengthens Frankfurt and Amsterdam as the place to receive shreds and forces every deshredder to be rewritten for the unified shred type; no vendor has published a Rotor plan.

The validator-side options are all dominated for this budget. A non-voting Frankfurt node costs €390–1,500/month with ~2 months of lead time on popular SKUs and receives shreds last as an unstaked node; a voting validator adds 0.8 SOL per epoch in admission tickets and needs millions of SOL to move its Turbine position or SWQoS share; Paladin's P3 lane is defunct (two validators, website blank); Harmonic has no trader-facing lane and is reached through the senders the operator already pays; Triton's Cascade stake marketplace was retired in February 2026 ([ERPC](https://erpc.global/en/news/2025/10/17/erpc-premium-metal-appplusplus-price-update/); [Solana Compass Paladin](https://solanacompass.com/projects/paladin); [Triton](https://blog.triton.one/swqos-for-everyone-why-we-retired-cascade-marketplace/)) [V/M]. A private `--staked-nodes-overrides` entry from a Frankfurt validator is one YAML line and technically trivial, but there is no public price and it requires a relationship ([Solana SWQoS guide](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/stake-weighted-qos)) [M].

## A ranked, costed plan: measure for free, buy one feed, build the TPU lane, then bid

The ordering principle is the internal finding that order is mostly arrival time (54–58% fee-order correlation across all trades) and that fee reorders only within a scheduling batch (73% among contested snipes): spend on colocation and path first, on free compute trimming second, on fees last (internal: [speed-tx-landing.md](../docs/research/speed-tx-landing.md) §8) [I]. Every item below is scoped to one Frankfurt box and a budget between a few hundred and a few thousand USD per month; sender tips are variable cost on top (at the internal p50 recipe about 0.004 SOL per win and 0.001 SOL per landed loss, so roughly 0.005 SOL per filled position).

## Concrete changes this implies for the Rust bot

The current design (Yellowstone gRPC at `processed`, one durable nonce per intent, fan-out to Helius Sender, Jito, Nozomi, Astralane and BlockRazor, per-mint compute limits, slot-based exits with `max_hold_slots = 5`) is the field's consensus architecture; the changes are about the 200 ms slot, the missing TPU lane, leader-class routing and the Alpenglow clock. The hard exit at creation slot + 5 was designed as ~2 s of wall clock at 400 ms slots and is now ~1 s; at 200 ms slots a slot is no longer a stable unit of time, and the exit policy in `rugsnipe-exit` should be re-expressed in milliseconds (or re-tuned to ~10 slots) and made aware of the 800 ms leader window.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. "Winning slot zero" is not a latency race against the creator's bundle, which cannot be beaten, nor against 200 competing bots, which the on-chain data does not show; it is a race among a handful of professional wallets for the first conflicting buy released when the bundle's write-lock on the bonding curve clears, decided first by whether your packet is already in the leader's queue and second by its fee-per-cost rank among the packets that are. Everything that is expensive in this market (premium shred feeds, senders, tips) buys at most one scheduling batch of advantage; everything that is free (being in the right Frankfurt facility, a direct QUIC connection, a 62k CU request with a tight data-size limit, a correctly sized `max_sol_cost`) buys as much or more. The operator's own data shows the field converting ~60% of the launches it wants into same-slot fills and losing most of the rest on slippage it set itself, which means the next edge is a pricing model, not a faster wire.

Two things should change how the operator thinks going forward. The 5–9 transaction gaps are evidence that some competitors act on signals that precede shreds, so BAM and Helius preconfirmations deserve a measured trial rather than dismissal, and the 21 October window, with BAM in maintenance and Firedancer out, is a natural experiment in which tip-ordered and fee-ordered regimes swap for a week; log it carefully. And because leader windows are now 800 ms and the next-slot fallback keeps the same leader three quarters of the time, a slot+1 fill with the same submit path is often worth more than a same-slot attempt at a rank-3 premium. Measure first; the bot already has the hard parts.
